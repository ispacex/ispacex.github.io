## Строфы 1–10 {#s1}

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">अथ</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Atha</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">तृतीयोऽधिकारः।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Tṛtīyo'dhikāraḥ|</span></p>

<p class="pv-en pv-anchor" id="s1-ru" lang="en"><ruby>And now<rp> (</rp><rt>atha</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>the third<rp> (</rp><rt>tṛtīyaḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>chapter<rp> (</rp><rt>adhikāraḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>|</p>

<hr class="pv-rule" />

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="v1" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">एवमुक्ता महादेवी जगदानन्दकारिणा।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Evamuktā mahādevī jagadānandakāriṇā|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">प्रणिपत्य पुनर्वाक्यमिदमाह जगत्पतिम्॥१॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Praṇipatya punarvākyamidamāha jagatpatim||1||</span></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">The Great Goddess <span class="pv-note">— Śakti —</span> <span class="pv-w">(mahā-devī)</span> <ruby>was thus spoken<rp> (</rp><rt>evam uktā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> by the One who produces Jagadānanda <span class="pv-note">— Śiva —</span> <span class="pv-w">(jagat-ānanda-kāriṇā)</span>. <ruby>After bowing down<rp> (</rp><rt>praṇipatya</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> to the Lord of the world <span class="pv-note">— to Śiva —</span> <span class="pv-w">(jagat-patim)</span>, <ruby>She again said<rp> (</rp><rt>punar... āha</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>these words<rp> (</rp><rt>vākyam idam</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>||1||</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">After the Great Lord spoke to the Great Goddess so in the second chapter, She opens here the third chapter called 'Mantroddhāra' (The selection or extraction of mantra-s) with more questions for Him. He produces<br />
Jagadānanda because gives the viewpoint in which the entire universe is perceived only as being Himself.</p>

<hr class="pv-rule" />

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="v2" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">एवमेतन्महादेव नान्यथा समुदाहृतम्। -</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Evametanmahādeva nānyathā samudāhṛtam|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">यथाख्यातं तथा ज्ञातमादितः समनुक्रमात्॥२॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Yathākhyātaṁ tathā jñātamāditaḥ samanukramāt||2||</span></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><ruby>Oh Great God<rp> (</rp><rt>mahā-deva</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>! <ruby>This<rp> (</rp><rt>etad</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> which has been declared <em>(by You)</em> <span class="pv-w">(samudāhṛtam)</span> <ruby>is like this<rp> (</rp><rt>evam</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(and)</em> <ruby>not otherwise<rp> (</rp><rt>na anyathā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. I have understood <span class="pv-note">— lit. understood —</span> <span class="pv-w">(jñātam)</span> <ruby>exactly<rp> (</rp><rt>yathā... tathā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> what You have said <span class="pv-note">— lit. said —</span> <span class="pv-w">(ākhyātam)</span> <ruby>in succession<rp> (</rp><rt>samanukramāt</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, from the <em>(very)</em> <ruby>beginning<rp> (</rp><rt>āditaḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <span class="pv-note">— i.e. I have understood all you said in the previous two chapters, from beginning to end —</span>||2||</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">Now Śakti confirms the Śiva's teachings in the previous chapter. Not only that, She says that She has understood everything from beginning to end.</p>

<hr class="pv-rule" />

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="v3" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">शिवादिवस्तुरूपाणां वाचकान्परमेश्वर।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Śivādivasturūpāṇāṁ vācakānparameśvara|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">साम्प्रतं श्रोतुमिच्छामि प्रसादाद्वक्तुमर्हसि॥३॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Sāmprataṁ śrotumicchāmi prasādādvaktumarhasi||3||</span></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><ruby>Oh Supreme Lord<rp> (</rp><rt>parama-īśvara</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>! <ruby>Now<rp> (</rp><rt>sāmpratam</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>I wish<rp> (</rp><rt>icchāmi</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>to listen<rp> (</rp><rt>śrotum</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> about the 'vācaka-s' or 'denoters' <span class="pv-note">— lit. to the 'vācaka-s' or 'denoters' —</span> <span class="pv-w">(vācakān)</span> <ruby>of the forms of realities such as Śiva, etc.<rp> (</rp><rt>śiva-ādi-vastu-rūpāṇām</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. Please, kindly speak <em>(about this subject-matter)</em> <span class="pv-w">(prasādāt vaktum arhasi)</span>||3||</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">Now She wants to receive teachings <ruby>about the 'mantra-s', which are always denoters<rp> (</rp><rt>subjects</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>and never something being denoted<rp> (</rp><rt>objects</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. She wants to know <ruby>about the mantra-s of Śiva, the three goddesses<rp> (</rp><rt>Parā, Parāparā and Aparā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, etc.</p>

<hr class="pv-rule" />

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="v4" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">इत्युक्तः स महेशान्या जगदार्तिहरो हरः।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Ityuktaḥ sa maheśānyā jagadārtiharo haraḥ|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">वाचकानवदन्मन्त्रान्पारम्पर्यक्रमागतान्॥४॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Vācakānavadanmantrānpāramparyakramāgatān||4||</span></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><ruby>He<rp> (</rp><rt>saḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, Hara <span class="pv-note">— Śiva —</span> <span class="pv-w">(haraḥ)</span> —<ruby>the Destroyer of pain in the world<rp> (</rp><rt>jagat-ārtiharo</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>—, <ruby>having been so said<rp> (</rp><rt>iti-uktaḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>by the Great Mistress<rp> (</rp><rt>mahā-īśānyā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, started to speak <span class="pv-note">— lit. spoke —</span> <span class="pv-w">(avadat)</span> <ruby>about the 'vācaka-s' or 'denoters'<rp> (</rp><rt>vācakān</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, i.e. <ruby>about the mantra-s<rp> (</rp><rt>mantrān</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> which come through an uninterrupted succession <em>(of Guru-s and disciples)</em> <span class="pv-w">(pāramparya-krama-āgatān)</span>||4||</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">And Śiva is then going to please the Great Goddess by means of a long dissertation about mantra-s. These mantra-s come, without any interruption, from immemorial time, through the eternal relationship of Guru-disciple. In other words, the Guru passes a mantra over to his disciple and the latter gets Liberation with this mantra. Next, this disciple becomes a Guru and hands the same mantra over to his own disciple, and so on and on.</p>

<hr class="pv-rule" />

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="v5" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">या सा शक्तिर्जगद्धातुः कथिता समवायिनी।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Yā sā śaktirjagaddhātuḥ kathitā samavāyinī|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">इच्छात्वं तस्य सा देवि सिसृक्षोः प्रतिपद्यते॥५॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Icchātvaṁ tasya sā devi sisṛkṣoḥ pratipadyate||5||</span></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><ruby>The Power<rp> (</rp><rt>sā śaktiḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>who<rp> (</rp><rt>yā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>belongs to the Creator<rp> (</rp><rt>dhātuḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>of the world<rp> (</rp><rt>jagat</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>is said to be<rp> (</rp><rt>kathitā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> inherent in <em>(Him)</em> <span class="pv-w">(samavāyinī)</span>. <ruby>Oh Goddess<rp> (</rp><rt>devi</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>She<rp> (</rp><rt>sā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>changes into the Will<rp> (</rp><rt>icchātvam... pratipadyate</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> of that <em>(Creator)</em> <span class="pv-w">(tasya)</span> <em>(when)</em> <ruby>He is desirous to manifest<rp> (</rp><rt>sisṛkṣoḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(the universe)</em>||5||</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">I explain the whole set of 12 stanzas from the 5th one to the 16th one under the 16th stanza.</p>

<hr class="pv-rule" />

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="v6" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">सैकापि सत्यनेकत्वं यथा गच्छति तच्छृणु।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Saikāpi satyanekatvaṁ yathā gacchati tacchṛṇu|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">एवमेतदिति ज्ञेयं नान्यथेति सुनिश्चितम्॥६॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Evametaditi jñeyaṁ nānyatheti suniścitam||6||</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">ज्ञापयन्ती जगत्यत्र ज्ञानशक्तिर्निगद्यते।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Jñāpayantī jagatyatra jñānaśaktirnigadyate|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">एवम्भूतमिदं वस्तु भवत्विति यदा पुनः॥७॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Evambhūtamidaṁ vastu bhavatviti yadā punaḥ||7||</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">जाता तदैव तत्तद्वत्कुर्वत्यत्र क्रियोच्यते।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Jātā tadaiva tattadvatkurvatyatra kriyocyate|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">एवं सैषा द्विरूपापि पुनर्भेदैरनेकताम्॥८॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Evaṁ saiṣā dvirūpāpi punarbhedairanekatām||8||</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">अर्थोपाधिवशाद्याति चिन्तामणिरिवेश्वरी।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Arthopādhivaśādyāti cintāmaṇiriveśvarī|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">तत्र तावत्समापन्ना मातृभावं विभिद्यते॥९॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Tatra tāvatsamāpannā mātṛbhāvaṁ vibhidyate||9||</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">द्विधा च नवधा चैव पञ्चाशद्धा च मालिनी।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Dvidhā ca navadhā caiva pañcāśaddhā ca mālinī|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">बीजयोन्यात्मकाद्भेदाद्द्विधा बीजं स्वरा मताः॥१०॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Bījayonyātmakādbhedāddvidhā bījaṁ svarā matāḥ||10||</span></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><ruby>Listen<rp> (</rp><rt>tad śṛṇu</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>how<rp> (</rp><rt>yathā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>She<rp> (</rp><rt>sā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>becomes<rp> (</rp><rt>gacchati</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>many<rp> (</rp><rt>anekatvam</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>even though<rp> (</rp><rt>api</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>She is<rp> (</rp><rt>satī</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>one<rp> (</rp><rt>ekā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>:</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><ruby>By announcing<rp> (</rp><rt>jñāpayantī</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>with determination<rp> (</rp><rt>suniścitam</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>that 'This<rp> (</rp><rt>etad</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>knowable or object<rp> (</rp><rt>jñeyam</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(is)</em> like this (evam... iti), <em>(and)</em> <ruby>not<rp> (</rp><rt>na</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>otherwise<rp> (</rp><rt>anyathā... iti</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>!<ruby>', She is called<rp> (</rp><rt>nigadyate</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>Power of Knowledge<rp> (</rp><rt>jñāna-śaktiḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> in this world (jagati atra).</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><ruby>However<rp> (</rp><rt>punar</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>when<rp> (</rp><rt>yadā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>She appears<rp> (</rp><rt>jātā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(like this:)</em> 'Let this thing become (idam vastu bhavatu) <ruby>so<rp> (</rp><rt>evam-bhūtam... iti</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>!<ruby>', then<rp> (</rp><rt>tadā eva</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>by making<rp> (</rp><rt>kurvatī</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> that <span class="pv-note">— the aforesaid thing —</span> <span class="pv-w">(tad)</span> <ruby>in this way<rp> (</rp><rt>tad-vat</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> here <span class="pv-note">— in this world —</span> <span class="pv-w">(atra)</span>, <ruby>She is said to be<rp> (</rp><rt>ucyate</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(the Power of)</em> <ruby>Action<rp> (</rp><rt>kriyā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><ruby>Thus<rp> (</rp><rt>evam</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>though<rp> (</rp><rt>api</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> this very <em>(Goddess)</em> <span class="pv-w">(sā eṣā)</span> has two <em>(main)</em> <ruby>forms<rp> (</rp><rt>dvi-rūpā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>She nevertheless undergoes<rp> (</rp><rt>punar... yāti</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>many<rp> (</rp><rt>anekatām</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>changes<rp> (</rp><rt>bhedaiḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>according to<rp> (</rp><rt>vaśāt</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>the characteristics<rp> (</rp><rt>upādhi</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> of the <em>(desired)</em> <ruby>objects<rp> (</rp><rt>artha</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. <em>(Consequently, this)</em> <ruby>Mistress<rp> (</rp><rt>īśvarī</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(is)</em> <ruby>like<rp> (</rp><rt>iva</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>a thought-gem<rp> (</rp><rt>cintā-maṇiḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <span class="pv-note">— which yields its possessor all desires —</span>.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><ruby>Then<rp> (</rp><rt>tatra</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <em>(when the Mistress)</em> <ruby>firstly assumes the condition<rp> (</rp><rt>tāvat samāpannā... bhāvam</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>of Mother<rp> (</rp><rt>mātṛ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>She is divided<rp> (</rp><rt>vibhidyate</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>into two ways<rp> (</rp><rt>dvidhā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> and (ca... ca eva) <ruby>in nine ways<rp> (</rp><rt>navadhā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>and<rp> (</rp><rt>ca</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(when such a Mistress is divided)</em> <ruby>in fifty ways<rp> (</rp><rt>pañcāśaddhā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <em>(She becomes)</em> <ruby>a wearer of a garland<rp> (</rp><rt>mālinī</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(made out of the fifty letters of the Sanskrit alphabet)</em>.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><ruby>With the division consisting<rp> (</rp><rt>ātmakāt bhedāt</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>of bīja<rp> (</rp><rt>bīja</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(and)</em> <ruby>yoni<rp> (</rp><rt>yoni</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>She appears in two ways<rp> (</rp><rt>dvidhā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. <ruby>The vowels<rp> (</rp><rt>svarāḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>are considered as<rp> (</rp><rt>matāḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>bīja or seed<rp> (</rp><rt>bījam</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>||6-10||</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">I explain the whole set of 12 stanzas from the 5th one to the 16th one under the 16th stanza.</p>

## Строфы 11–24 {#s2}

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="s2-sa" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">कादिभिश्च स्मृता योनिर्नवधा वर्गभेदतः।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Kādibhiśca smṛtā yonirnavadhā vargabhedataḥ|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">प्रतिवर्णविभेदेन शतार्धकिरणोज्ज्वला॥११॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Prativarṇavibhedena śatārdhakiraṇojjvalā||11||</span></p>

<p class="pv-en pv-anchor" id="s2-ru" lang="en"><ruby>Beginning with ka, etc.<rp> (</rp><rt>ka-ādibhiḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>She is said to be<rp> (</rp><rt>smṛtā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>yoni or womb<rp> (</rp><rt>yoniḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <span class="pv-note">— i.e. the consonants are regarded as yoni —</span>. <em>(Therefore,)</em> <ruby>according to the division<rp> (</rp><rt>bhedatas</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>of the varga-s or groups of letters<rp> (</rp><rt>varga</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <em>(She appears)</em> <ruby>in nine ways<rp> (</rp><rt>navadhā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. <em>(And)</em> <ruby>according to the division<rp> (</rp><rt>vibhedena</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>of each of letters<rp> (</rp><rt>prati-varṇa</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>She shines<rp> (</rp><rt>ujjvalā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>as fifty<rp> (</rp><rt>śata-ardha</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>rays<rp> (</rp><rt>kiraṇa</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <span class="pv-note">— since there are fifty letters in the Sanskrit alphabet —</span>||11||</p>

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="v16" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">तद्वदेव च शक्तीनां तत्सङ्ख्यानमनुक्रमात्।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Tadvadeva ca śaktīnāṁ tatsaṅkhyānamanukramāt|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">सर्वं च कथयिष्यामि तासां भेदं यथा शृणु॥१६॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Sarvaṁ ca kathayiṣyāmi tāsāṁ bhedaṁ yathā śṛṇu||16||</span></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><ruby>And<rp> (</rp><rt>ca</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, in the same way (tad-vat eva), <em>(She was made fiftyfold by the Highest Lord again, as being indicator)</em> of the śakti-s <em>(of such Rudra-s)</em> <span class="pv-note">— viz. the celebrated Rudrāṇī-s —</span> <span class="pv-w">(śaktīnām)</span> —<em>(whose quantity amounts)</em> to that number <span class="pv-note">— i.e. to fifty —</span> <span class="pv-w">(tad-saṅkhyānam)</span> <span class="pv-note">— Sanskrit structure here is a little far-fetched anyway —</span>— <ruby>in succession<rp> (</rp><rt>anukramāt</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. <em>(Now,)</em> <ruby>I will mention<rp> (</rp><rt>kathayiṣyāmi</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(the names of)</em> all <em>(the Rudra-s)</em> (sarvam ca) <ruby>in accordance with<rp> (</rp><rt>yathā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> their <span class="pv-note">— of the śakti-s or powers —</span> <span class="pv-w">(tāsām)</span> <ruby>division<rp> (</rp><rt>bhedam</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. <ruby>Listen<rp> (</rp><rt>śṛṇu</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>!||16||</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><ruby>The Power of the Great Lord<rp> (</rp><rt>i.e. Śakti</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>is inherent in Him<rp> (</rp><rt>i.e. in Śiva</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, viz. She 'naturally' belongs to Him. There is nothing artificial in this. If you get Him, you get Her, and if you get Her then you get Him too. When He wishes to manifest the universe, She becomes His Icchāśakti (Power of Will) in order to proceed to such universal manifestation. Next, She becomes His Jñānaśakti (Power of Knowledge) by feeling: 'This knowable or object is like this and not otherwise!'. Finally, when She is trying to actualize the universe which was only an idea in the Mind of the Supreme Self, She becomes His Kriyāśakti (Power of Action) in the process. So, She feels: 'Let this thing become so!'.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">And after this first creative movement, She has to assume all the aspects of the creation. In this way, She becomes a gazillion of things. But anyway, She never became anything, <ruby>because She is totally one with Her Lord<rp> (</rp><rt>with Śiva</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. She is called 'thought-gem' since She fulfills all the desires Her Master has, i.e. She manifests for Him all the objects which He desired, as it were.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">Next, when She becomes Mātṛkā (the mysterious un-understood Mother of all the letters), She is divided in two ways, viz. into vowel and consonant. A vowel is called 'bīja' or 'seed' because it fertilizes the 'yoni' (lit. womb, uterus), that is, the consonant. This is clearly understood if you consider the consonants in this light: You cannot pronounce them without the help of a vowel. For example, 'k'... how to pronounce it properly without the help of a vowel? It is not possible to adequately utter it without the support of a vowel. For instance: 'ka'... now it is easily utterable. So, vowels are seeds which make the consonants or wombs fruitful. Without vowels, the consonants would be just like barren women, unable to procreate. But anyway, the vowels can be pronounced by themselves, that is, without the help of any consonant. All in all, vowels are divine while consonants (in general) are not divine.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">Śiva is vowel and Śakti is consonant in this scheme. Yes, everything is Śiva in the end, because Śiva and Śakti are one. This one Reality is divided into two only for the purpose of being studied then.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">The Sanskrit alphabet in its traditional form contains 8 varga-s or groups of letters:</p>

1. A-varga (Vowels), whose presiding deity is Yogīśī or Yogīśvarī or Mahālakṣmī
2. Ka-varga (Gutturals), whose presiding deity is Brāhmaṇī or Brāhmī
3. Ca-varga (Palatals), whose presiding deity is Māheśī or Māheśvarī
4. Ṭa-varga (Cerebrals), whose presiding deity is Kaumārī
5. Ta-varga (Dentals), whose presiding deity is Vaiṣṇavī
6. Pa-varga (Labials), whose presiding deity is Yāmyā or Vārāhī
7. Ya-varga (Semivowels), whose presiding deity is Aindrī or Indrāṇī
8. Śa-varga (Sibilants, Sonant Aspirate + Kūṭabīja --the conjunct kṣa formed from 'ka + sa' but very often considered to be a letter in itself--), whose presiding deity is Cāmuṇḍā

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">Now, the alphabet presented in its <ruby>traditional form 'does not' include Kūṭabīja<rp> (</rp><rt>the letter kṣa</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, but when it does, Kūṭabīja is <ruby>usually assigned to the eighth varga or group<rp> (</rp><rt>Śa-varga</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. <p class="pv-en" lang="en">Now the Kṣa-varga constitutes the brand-new 9th varga. When Śakti appears in this way, a new deity arises: Śiva-Śakti. This goddess will become the presiding deity of the A-varga (the group of vowels), and the other goddess (the one previously presiding over the A-varga) becomes the presiding deity of the new Kṣa-varga. Look again at the previous arrangement (if the Sanskrit alphabet is divided in 8 varga-s):</p>

1. Yogīśī or Yogīśvarī or Mahālakṣmī (presiding over the A-varga)
2. Brāhmaṇī or Brāhmī (presiding over the Ka-varga)
3. Māheśī or Māheśvarī (presiding over the Ca-varga)
4. Kaumārī (presiding over the Ṭa-varga)
5. Vaiṣṇavī (presiding over the Ta-varga)
6. Yāmyā or Vārāhī (presiding over the Pa-varga)
7. Aindrī or Indrāṇī (presiding over the Ya-varga)
8. Cāmuṇḍā (presiding over the Śa-varga)

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">But if the Kṣa-varga (the ninth group of letters) is created, then the order slightly changes (I highlighted in bold letters the change):</p>

1. **Śiva-Śakti (presiding over the A-varga)**
2. Brāhmaṇī or Brāhmī (presiding over the Ka-varga)
3. Māheśī or Māheśvarī (presiding over the Ca-varga)
4. Kaumārī (presiding over the Ṭa-varga)
5. Vaiṣṇavī (presiding over the Ta-varga)
6. Yāmyā or Vārāhī (presiding over the Pa-varga)
7. Aindrī or Indrāṇī (presiding over the Ya-varga)
8. Cāmuṇḍā (presiding over the Śa-varga)
9. **Yogīśī or Yogīśvarī or Mahālakṣmī (presiding over the Kṣa-varga)**

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">And because She shines as 50 rays, these 9 varga-s or groups of letters contain 50 letters. This is the sense.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">Śakti, the Power of Śiva or Śambhu, is called the denoting One in the scriptures. This is because She is the Source of all the mantra-s (the denoters or subjects). That is why when one repeats a mantra, his goal is Śakti and not Śiva. But again, when you get Śakti you get Śiva. So, in the end, the mantra method ends in formless Śiva. Śakti is the Face of Śiva, i.e. She makes Him have a form which can be realized by the sincere spiritual aspirants.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">Now, in the stanza 13, Śiva analyzes the last 8 varga-s or groups of letters out of the 9 varga-s already enunciated (the vowels are excluded here). And not only this but He also says that the set of eight groups of letters is known as Aghora, etc. in succession. What is this? I will explain it to you:</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">Śiva is referring to the male consorts of the śakti-s mentioned in the upcoming Parāparā's mantra (which will be explained later in this chapter). These male consorts, if my memory serves me <ruby>well, are the 8 Vijñānākala-s awakened by Śiva<rp> (</rp><rt>see Mālinīvijayottaratantra I, 18-21</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. In turn, these male consorts have eight associate <ruby>deities, which were already mentioned above<rp> (</rp><rt>i.e. Māheśī or Māheśvarī, Brāhmaṇī or Brāhmī, etc.</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> but the goddesses Brāhmaṇī or Brāhmī and Māheśī or Māheśvarī along with Yāmyā or Vārāhī and Aindrī or Indrāṇī exchange their respective positions now. So, according to this teaching, the eight groups of letters would look like this:</p>

1. **Śiva-Śakti (presiding over the A-varga - Vowels)** ------> **Śambhu** (this group is excluded in the stanza 13)
2. **Māheśī or Māheśvarī** (presiding over the Ka-varga) ------> **Aghora**
3. **Brāhmaṇī or Brāhmī** (presiding over the Ca-varga) ------> **Paramaghora**
4. Kaumārī (presiding over the Ṭa-varga) ------> **Ghorarūpa (or Ghora, plainly)**
5. Vaiṣṇavī (presiding over the Ta-varga) ------> **Ghoramukha**
6. **Aindrī or Indrāṇī** (presiding over the Pa-varga) ------> **Bhīma**
7. **Yāmyā or Vārāhī** (presiding over the Ya-varga) ------> **Bhīṣaṇa**
8. Cāmuṇḍā (presiding over the Śa-varga) ------> **Vamana**
9. Yogīśī or Yogīśvarī or Mahālakṣmī (presiding over the Kṣa-varga) ------> **Pibana (or Pivana)**

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">Now this should look a little more clear for you.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">Regarding the stanza 14, I want to make this point very clear:</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">The order given in the stanza 14 for the 8 groups of letters (not the 9 ones in this case) is not the real order of the deities with reference to the respective varga-s or groups of letters. <ruby>In the stanza, the author<rp> (</rp><rt>Śiva</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> arranged the names of the goddesses in a way which meets the meter requirements. What do I mean by all this? First, the order given in the stanza is as follows:</p>

1. Māheśī or Māheśvarī
2. Brāhmaṇī or Brāhmī
3. Kaumārī
4. Vaiṣṇavī
5. Aindrī or Indrāṇī
6. Yāmyā or Vārāhī
7. Cāmuṇḍā
8. Yogīśī or Yogīśvarī or Mahālakṣmī

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">But the real order, according to the traditional way, is like this:</p>

1. Yogīśī or Yogīśvarī or Mahālakṣmī
2. Brāhmaṇī or Brāhmī
3. Māheśī or Māheśvarī
4. Kaumārī
5. Vaiṣṇavī
6. Yāmyā or Vārāhī
7. Aindrī or Indrāṇī
8. Cāmuṇḍā

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">I will only give the names of the <ruby>Rudra-s, because the names of their consorts<rp> (</rp><rt>the Rudrāṇī-s</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> are easily created by transforming the masculine noun <ruby>being assigned as a name into its feminine form<rp> (</rp><rt>e.g. Jaya ---> Jayā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. These Rudra-s and their wives are eternally liberated, i.e. they never got Liberation but they were created like this, liberated from the start:</p>

## Строфы 25–30 {#s3}

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="s3-sa" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">बीजयोनिसमुद्भूता रुद्रशक्तिसमाश्रयाः।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Bījayonisamudbhūtā rudraśaktisamāśrayāḥ|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">वाचकानामनन्तत्वात्परिसङ्ख्या न विद्यते॥२५॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Vācakānāmanantatvātparisaṅkhyā na vidyate||25||</span></p>

<p class="pv-en pv-anchor" id="s3-ru" lang="en"><em>(Since the vācaka-s or denoters, i.e. the mantra-s)</em> have arisen from the seeds and the wombs <span class="pv-note">— the vowels and the consonants —</span> <span class="pv-w">(bīja-yoni-samudbhūtāḥ)</span>, they reside in Rudra <span class="pv-note">— Śiva —</span> and <em>(His)</em> Power <span class="pv-note">— Śakti —</span> <span class="pv-w">(rudra-śakti-samāśrayāḥ)</span>. <em>(Therefore,)</em> there is no (na vidyate) <ruby>limit to the number<rp> (</rp><rt>parisaṅkhyā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> of vācaka-s or denoters <span class="pv-note">— mantra-s —</span> <span class="pv-w">(vācakānām)</span> <ruby>as they are infinite<rp> (</rp><rt>anantatvāt</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>||25||</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">Since Śiva and Śakti are the residences of all these mantra-s, there is no limit to their number. This is very simple to understand.</p>

<hr class="pv-rule" />

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="v26" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">सर्वशास्त्रार्थगर्भिण्या इत्येवं विधयानया।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Sarvaśāstrārthagarbhiṇyā ityevaṁ vidhayānayā|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">अघोरं बोधयामास स्वेच्छया परमेश्वरः॥२६॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Aghoraṁ bodhayāmāsa svecchayā parameśvaraḥ||26||</span></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><ruby>Thus<rp> (</rp><rt>iti-evam</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>the Supreme Lord<rp> (</rp><rt>parama-īśvaraḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>of His own Free Will<rp> (</rp><rt>sva-icchayā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>awakened<rp> (</rp><rt>bodhayāmāsa</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>Aghora<rp> (</rp><rt>aghoram</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>by means of this method<rp> (</rp><rt>vidhayā anayā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> which is <ruby>replete with the meaning of all the scriptures<rp> (</rp><rt>sarva-śāstra-artha-garbhiṇyā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>||26||</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">By means of this method, i.e. by means of His teachings. Aghora is another name for Anantabhaṭṭhāraka, the chief of all the Mantra-s <span class="pv-w">(experients or knowers of Sadvidyā <span class="pv-note">— tattva or category 5 —</span>)</span> who manifested 70 million mantra-s.</p>

<hr class="pv-rule" />

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="v27" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">स तया सम्प्रबुद्धः सन्योनिं विक्षोभ्य शक्तिभिः।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Sa tayā samprabuddhaḥ sanyoniṁ vikṣobhya śaktibhiḥ|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">तत्समानश्रुतीन्वर्णांस्तत्सङ्ख्यानसृजत्प्रभुः॥२७॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Tatsamānaśrutīnvarṇāṁstatsaṅkhyānasṛjatprabhuḥ||27||</span></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">He <span class="pv-note">— Aghora —</span> <span class="pv-w">(saḥ)</span>, the powerful lord <em>(of the Mantra-s)</em> <span class="pv-w">(prabhuḥ)</span>, <ruby>being completely awakened<rp> (</rp><rt>samprabuddhaḥ san</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> by it <span class="pv-note">— by the method of Śiva —</span> <span class="pv-w">(tayā)</span>, <ruby>after agitating<rp> (</rp><rt>vikṣobhya</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> the Womb <span class="pv-note">— the Śakti or Power of Śiva, i.e. His Svātantryaśakti or Power of Absolute Freedom —</span> <span class="pv-w">(yonim)</span> with the <em>(very Śiva's)</em> <ruby>powers<rp> (</rp><rt>śaktibhiḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>brought about<rp> (</rp><rt>asṛjat</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>the letters<rp> (</rp><rt>varṇān</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> of the same number <span class="pv-note">— viz. 50, as the number of the Rudra-s —</span> <span class="pv-w">(tad-saṅkhyān)</span> <em>(and)</em> the <em>(respective)</em> sounds <em>(of such letters, which are also)</em> of the same number <span class="pv-note">— i.e. 50 —</span> <span class="pv-w">(tat-samāna-śrutīn)</span>||27||</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">So, it was Aghora who made Śakti become the 50 letters of the Sanskrit alphabet along with their respective sounds. He did so by agitating the Power of the Lord, i.e. His Svātantryaśakti or Power of Absolute Freedom.</p>

<hr class="pv-rule" />

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="v28" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">ते तैरालिङ्गिताः सन्तः सर्वकामफलप्रदाः।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Te tairāliṅgitāḥ santaḥ sarvakāmaphalapradāḥ|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">भवन्ति साधकेन्द्राणां नान्यथा वीरवन्दिते॥२८॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Bhavanti sādhakendrāṇāṁ nānyathā vīravandite||28||</span></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><ruby>Oh You who are extolled by the Heroes<rp> (</rp><rt>vīra-vandite</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, those <em>(50 letters)</em> <span class="pv-w">(te)</span>, <ruby>being embraced<rp> (</rp><rt>āliṅgitāḥ santaḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> by them <span class="pv-note">— by Aghora and the rest of Mantra-s (the knowers themselves) —</span> <span class="pv-w">(taiḥ)</span>, <ruby>become<rp> (</rp><rt>bhavanti</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>the bestowers of the fruits of all the desires<rp> (</rp><rt>sarva-kāma-phala-pradāḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>with reference to the best adepts<rp> (</rp><rt>sādhaka-indrāṇām</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <em>(and)</em> <ruby>this is not otherwise<rp> (</rp><rt>na anyathā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>||28||</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">Mātṛkā (the mysterious un-understood Mother) is Śakti as the source of all letters. When the disciple finally understands this point, viz. that all his thoughts and words <ruby>are just the manifestation of the Power of Śiva<rp> (</rp><rt>His Śakti</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, when he finally realizes that all these things are not different from Śakti, then at that time, Mātṛkā stops being Mātṛkā (the un-understood Mother) and becomes Mātṛ (the Mother) or the One who provides him with infinite Bliss. And this is not otherwise. According to Trika Shaivism there are three means to the attainment of Liberation: Śāmbhavopāya, Śāktopāya and Āṇavopāya. This process of finally understanding the un-understood Mother happens at the end of Śāktopāya, which directly sends the disciple to Śāmbhavopāya. When Śāmbhavopāya is attained, then Liberation is practically at hand.</p>

<hr class="pv-rule" />

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="v29" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">तैरिदं सन्ततं विश्वं सदेवासुरमानुषम्।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Tairidaṁ santataṁ viśvaṁ sadevāsuramānuṣam|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">तेभ्यः शास्त्राणि वेदाश्च सम्भवन्ति पुनः पुनः॥२९॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Tebhyaḥ śāstrāṇi vedāśca sambhavanti punaḥ punaḥ||29||</span></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><ruby>This<rp> (</rp><rt>idam</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>universe<rp> (</rp><rt>viśvam</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>containing gods, demons and human beings<rp> (</rp><rt>sa-deva-asura-mānuṣam</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(is)</em> <ruby>uninterruptedly<rp> (</rp><rt>santatam</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(pervaded)</em> by them <span class="pv-note">— by such letters —</span> <span class="pv-w">(taiḥ)</span>. <em>(In the same way, Tantric)</em> <ruby>scriptures<rp> (</rp><rt>śāstrāṇi</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>and<rp> (</rp><rt>ca</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>Veda-s<rp> (</rp><rt>vedāḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>arise<rp> (</rp><rt>sambhavanti</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>from them<rp> (</rp><rt>tebhyaḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> over and over again (punar punar)||29||</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">All the living beings in this universe are pervaded by such letters. What does it mean? It means this, for example: 'You are so intelligent!', and you feel very well, or 'You are such a fool!', and you feel very bad. When someone can stay undisturbed in the face of this kind of phrases, that person is not pervaded by letters, i.e. he is liberated. So, can you use this method to detect liberated people? Not really, because that divine person knows what you are doing and can cheat you back by behaving himself like a common person, i.e. acting as if pervaded by praises or insults. Liberated ones are then known by other liberated ones and not otherwise.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">And all the scriptures, which consist of letters forming words, sentences, etc., are obviously arising from letters again and again. This is very easy to understand.</p>

<hr class="pv-rule" />

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="v30" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">अनन्तस्यापि भेदस्य शिवशक्तेर्महात्मनः।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Anantasyāpi bhedasya śivaśaktermahātmanaḥ|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">कार्यभेदान्महादेवि त्रैविध्यं समुदाहृतम्॥३०॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Kāryabhedānmahādevi traividhyaṁ samudāhṛtam||30||</span></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><ruby>Oh Great Goddess<rp> (</rp><rt>mahā-devi</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>according to the divisions of effects<rp> (</rp><rt>kārya-bhedāt</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>it is said<rp> (</rp><rt>samudāhṛtam</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> that there are three portions <span class="pv-note">— lit. threefold —</span> <span class="pv-w">(traividhyam)</span> <ruby>in the Great Being<rp> (</rp><rt>mahā-ātmanaḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> of the Śakti <span class="pv-note">— Power —</span> <ruby>of Śiva<rp> (</rp><rt>śiva-śakteḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>even if<rp> (</rp><rt>api</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> It <span class="pv-note">— the Great Being of the Śakti of Śiva —</span> contains infinite divisions (anantasya... bhedasya)||30||</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><ruby>According to the divisions of 'kārya'<rp> (</rp><rt>effects</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, i.e. according to the divisions of the three kinds of effects (non-dualism, non-dualism/dualism and dualism). These three portions are the three celebrated Aghorā-s, Ghorā-s and Ghoratarī-s powers, which are nothing else than Parā, Parāparā and Aparā powers. So, despite the Power of the Great Lord contains innumerable divisions, it is said that there are three main manifestations of Hers.</p>

## Строфы 31–41 {#s4}

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="s4-sa" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">विषयेष्वेव संलीनानधोऽधः पातयन्त्यणून्।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Viṣayeṣveva saṁlīnānadho'dhaḥ pātayantyaṇūn|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">रुद्राणून्याः समालिङ्ग्य घोरतर्योऽपराः स्मृताः॥३१॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Rudrāṇūnyāḥ samāliṅgya ghorataryo'parāḥ smṛtāḥ||31||</span></p>

<p class="pv-en pv-anchor" id="s4-ru" lang="en">The Ghoratarī-s <span class="pv-note">— lit. very terrible —</span> <span class="pv-w">(ghorataryaḥ)</span> <ruby>who<rp> (</rp><rt>yāḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>by embracing<rp> (</rp><rt>samāliṅgya</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> the Rudra souls <span class="pv-note">— liberated souls —</span> <span class="pv-w">(rudra-aṇūn)</span>, <ruby>throw<rp> (</rp><rt>pātayanti</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>the individual souls<rp> (</rp><rt>aṇūn</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>who are clung to<rp> (</rp><rt>saṁlīnān</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>objects<rp> (</rp><rt>viṣayeṣu eva</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> down and down (adhas adhas), <ruby>are called<rp> (</rp><rt>smṛtāḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>Aparā-s or non-supreme ones<rp> (</rp><rt>aparāḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>||31||</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">These are Aparā powers <span class="pv-note">— non-supreme powers as they create only duality —</span>. They only love liberated people but hate the rest. They are extremely dangerous if you lack knowledge about the Self, because they will throw <ruby>you down and down if your Śiva's viewpoint<rp> (</rp><rt>i.e. 'All is Śiva!'</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> fails or is completely absent (as in the case of the ignorant people).</p>

<hr class="pv-rule" />

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="v32" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">मिश्रकर्मफलासक्तिं पूर्ववज्जनयन्ति याः।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Miśrakarmaphalāsaktiṁ pūrvavajjanayanti yāḥ|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">मुक्तिमार्गनिरोधिन्यस्ताः स्युर्घोराः परापराः॥३२॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Muktimārganirodhinyastāḥ syurghorāḥ parāparāḥ||32||</span></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">The Ghorā-s <span class="pv-note">— lit. terrible ones —</span> <span class="pv-w">(tāḥ... ghorāḥ)</span> <ruby>who<rp> (</rp><rt>yāḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>by generating<rp> (</rp><rt>janayanti</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>as before<rp> (</rp><rt>pūrvavat</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>attachment<rp> (</rp><rt>āsaktim</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>to the fruits<rp> (</rp><rt>phala</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> of mixed actions <span class="pv-note">— mixture of good and bad actions —</span> <span class="pv-w">(miśra-karma)</span>, <ruby>block<rp> (</rp><rt>nirodhinyaḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>the path<rp> (</rp><rt>mārga</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>to Liberation<rp> (</rp><rt>mukti</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>are<rp> (</rp><rt>syuḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> Parāparā-s <span class="pv-note">— partly supreme and partly non-supreme —</span> <span class="pv-w">(parā-aparāḥ)</span>||32||</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">These are Parāparā powers as they are a mixture of supreme and non-supreme, i.e. they are partly non-dualistic and partly dualistic. These powers are specially dangerous in the case of spiritual aspirants since they generate attachment to the fruits of good and bad actions (e.g. 'I am not advancing in Yoga', in this case, the aspirant is attached to his actions and respective fruits, hence he is worried about the final outcome of his efforts). In this smart way, they block the path to Liberation very effectively.</p>

<hr class="pv-rule" />

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="v33" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">पूर्ववज्जन्तुजातस्य शिवधामफलप्रदाः।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Pūrvavajjantujātasya śivadhāmaphalapradāḥ|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">पराः प्रकथितास्तज्ज्ञैरघोराः शिवशक्तयः॥३३॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Parāḥ prakathitāstajjñairaghorāḥ śivaśaktayaḥ||33||</span></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">The Aghorā-s <span class="pv-note">— lit. not terrible ones —</span> <span class="pv-w">(aghorāḥ)</span> <em>(are)</em> <ruby>the powers of Śiva<rp> (</rp><rt>śiva-śaktayaḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(who)</em> <ruby>bestow<rp> (</rp><rt>pradāḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>as before<rp> (</rp><rt>pūrvavat</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>the fruit<rp> (</rp><rt>phala</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>of the State of Śiva<rp> (</rp><rt>śiva-dhāma</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>on the multitude<rp> (</rp><rt>jātasya</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> of limited beings <span class="pv-note">— lit. insects —</span> <span class="pv-w">(jantu)</span>. <ruby>They are eminently called<rp> (</rp><rt>prakathitāḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>Parā-s or supreme ones<rp> (</rp><rt>parāḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> by the Knowers of That <span class="pv-note">— i.e. by the Knowers of the Supreme Principle —</span> <span class="pv-w">(tad-jñaiḥ)</span>||33||</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">These are the powers who bestow, in His name, Liberation on the multitude of conditioned individuals who are all the time afflicted by the massive sickness known as Saṁsāra (Transmigration full of misery). The sages call them Parā powers as they are supreme.</p>

<hr class="pv-rule" />

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="v34" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">एताः सर्वाणुसङ्घातमपि निष्ठा यथा स्थिताः।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Etāḥ sarvāṇusaṅghātamapi niṣṭhā yathā sthitāḥ|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">तथा ते कथिताः शम्भोः शक्तिरेकैव शाङ्करी॥३४॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Tathā te kathitāḥ śambhoḥ śaktirekaiva śāṅkarī||34||</span></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><ruby>As<rp> (</rp><rt>yathā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> these <em>(three kinds of śakti-s)</em> <span class="pv-w">(etāḥ)</span> <ruby>are devoted<rp> (</rp><rt>niṣṭhāḥ... sthitāḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> to the whole collection of aṇu-s <span class="pv-note">— i.e. words in first, second and third persons —</span> <span class="pv-w">(sarva-aṇu-saṅghātam api)</span>, <ruby>therefore<rp> (</rp><rt>tathā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>they have been spoken<rp> (</rp><rt>kathitāḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>to You<rp> (</rp><rt>te</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(by Me. Nevertheless,)</em> <ruby>Śāṅkarī<rp> (</rp><rt>śāṅkarī</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>the Power<rp> (</rp><rt>śaktiḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> of Śambhu <span class="pv-note">— of Śiva —</span> <span class="pv-w">(śambhoḥ)</span> <em>(is)</em> <ruby>only<rp> (</rp><rt>eva</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>one<rp> (</rp><rt>ekā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>||34||</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">The <ruby>term 'words in first person' indicates 'Śiva'<rp> (</rp><rt>Śivatattva and Śaktitattva - categories 1 and 2</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. The <ruby>term 'words in second person' indicates 'Śakti'<rp> (</rp><rt>Sadāśivatattva, Īśvaratattva and Sadvidyātattva - categories 3, 4 and 5</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. Finally, the <ruby>term 'words in third person' indicates 'nara'<rp> (</rp><rt>from Māyātattva down to Pṛthivītattva - categories 6 to 36</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. Abhinavagupta explains this very well in his Parātrīśikāvivaraṇa here and here. Anyway, in spite of all Her never-ending divisions and expansions, one should always remember that Śakti is only 'one'.</p>

<hr class="pv-rule" />

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="v35" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">अस्या वाचकभेदेन भेदोऽन्यः सम्प्रचक्ष्यते।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Asyā vācakabhedena bhedo'nyaḥ sampracakṣyate|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">यथेष्टफलसंसिद्ध्यै मन्त्रातन्त्रानुवर्तिनाम्॥३५॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Yatheṣṭaphalasaṁsiddhyai mantrātantrānuvartinām||35||</span></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><ruby>In order to achieve<rp> (</rp><rt>siddhyai</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>the desired<rp> (</rp><rt>yathā-iṣṭa</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>fruit<rp> (</rp><rt>phala</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>in the case of the unrestrained followers<rp> (</rp><rt>atantra-anuvartinām</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>of Mantra<rp> (</rp><rt>mantra</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>another<rp> (</rp><rt>anyaḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>division<rp> (</rp><rt>bhedaḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> of these <em>(śakti-s)</em> <span class="pv-w">(asyāḥ)</span> <ruby>is explained<rp> (</rp><rt>sampracakṣyate</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> according to the division of vācaka or denoter <span class="pv-note">— i.e. mantra —</span> <span class="pv-w">(vācaka-bhedena)</span>||35||</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">This division into the three abovementioned śakti-s will undergo even another division soon.</p>

<hr class="pv-rule" />

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="v36" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">विशेषविधिहीनेषु न्यासकर्मसु मन्त्रवित्।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Viśeṣavidhihīneṣu nyāsakarmasu mantravit|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">न्यसेच्छाक्तशरीरार्थं भिन्नयोनिं तु मालिनीम्॥३६॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Nyasecchāktaśarīrārthaṁ bhinnayoniṁ tu mālinīm||36||</span></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">With reference to the acts of 'nyāsa' devoid of <em>(any)</em> <ruby>particular method<rp> (</rp><rt>viśeṣa-vidhi-hīneṣu nyāsa-karmasu</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, the knower of the mantra-s <span class="pv-note">— or 'the knower of the Mantra, i.e. of I-consciousness' —</span> <span class="pv-w">(mantravit)</span> <ruby>should certainly perform the nyāsa<rp> (</rp><rt>nyāset... tu</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>of Mālinī<rp> (</rp><rt>mālinīm</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, which contains consonants <em>(and vowels)</em> <ruby>mixed<rp> (</rp><rt>bhinna-yoni</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>for the sake of<rp> (</rp><rt>artham</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>the Body<rp> (</rp><rt>śarīra</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>of Śakti<rp> (</rp><rt>śākta... iti</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>||36||</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">Nyāsa is the act of placing one's own hands on different parts of body while repeating mantra-s. The purpose in this has to do with attaining identification with particular deities, or else, with achieving full identification between the macrocosm and the microcosm, etc. Here Mālinīnyāsa <span class="pv-w">(the nyāsa in which you place the hands on different parts of your own body while repeating the letters of the Sanskrit alphabet arranged in the Mālinī way, i.e. with vowels and consonants mixing with each other)</span> is recommended. While the well-known Mātṛkā arrangement of the Sanskrit alphabet is based on separating vowels from consonants as shown in my explanation under the stanza 16 in this chapter, i.e. like this:</p>

<p class="pv-iast"><strong>a ā i ī u ū ṛ ṝ ḷ ḹ e ai o au aṁ aḥ</strong><br />
<strong>ka kha ga gha ṅa ca cha ja jha ña ṭa ṭha ḍa ḍha ṇa ta tha da dha na pa pha ba bha ma ya ra la va śa ṣa sa ha kṣa</strong></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">the Mālinī arrangement joins vowels and consonants together, i.e. it mixes them like this:</p>

<p class="pv-iast"><strong>na ṛ ṝ ḷ ḹ tha ca dha ī ṇa u ū ba ka kha ga gha ṅa i a va bha ya ḍa ḍha ṭha jha ña ja ra ṭa pa cha la ā sa aḥ ha ṣa kṣa ma śa aṁ ta e ai o au da pha</strong></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">In a nutshell, in the explanation of Mālinīnyāsa starting in the next stanza, each of these letters in this specific order will be associated with different areas of the body.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">And all this is always for the sake of the Body of Śakti, viz. the final goal <ruby>in doing nyāsa is to attain the Śakti aspect<rp> (</rp><rt>Sadāśivatattva, Īśvaratattva and Sadvidyā tattva-s</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>and not to attain either the Śiva aspect<rp> (</rp><rt>Śivatattva and Śaktitattva</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>or the nara aspect<rp> (</rp><rt>tattva-s from Māyā down to Pṛthivī</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. This is so because the root of all mantra-s is Śakti and not Śiva or nara. This root is Aham or I-consciousness. All in all, when one repeats a mantra, the attention is always going in the end to the root of the mantra, i.e. I-consciousness. In other words, the goal in repeating mantra-s is to realize this Śakti aspect or I-consciousness. This is the meaning.</p>

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="v39" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">ठो हस्तयोर्झञौ शाखा ज्रटौ शूलकपालके।</span><br />

'Pa' <em>(is assigned)</em> <ruby>to the heart<rp> (</rp><rt>pa-hṛd</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>'cha' and 'la'<rp> (</rp><rt>cha-lau</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(are assigned)</em> to both portions of the chest <span class="pv-note">— right and left ones —</span> <span class="pv-w">(stanau)</span>, <ruby>'ā'<rp> (</rp><rt>ā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(is assigned)</em> <ruby>to the milk<rp> (</rp><rt>kṣīram</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>'sa'<rp> (</rp><rt>sa</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(is assigned)</em> <ruby>to Self<rp> (</rp><rt>jīvaḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <em>(and)</em> visarga <span class="pv-note">— i.e. 'aḥ' —</span> <em>(is assigned)</em> <ruby>to the general prāṇa<rp> (</rp><rt>visarga-yuk</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. The next one <span class="pv-note">— i.e. 'ha' —</span> <span class="pv-w">(tatparaḥ)</span> <ruby>is said to be<rp> (</rp><rt>kathitaḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(assigned to)</em> the <em>(special)</em> <ruby>prāṇa<rp> (</rp><rt>prāṇaḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. <ruby>'Ṣa' and 'kṣa'<rp> (</rp><rt>ṣa-kṣau</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>remain in belly and navel<rp> (</rp><rt>udara-nābhi-gau</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. <ruby>'Ma', 'śa', 'aṁ' and 'ta'<rp> (</rp><rt>ma-śa-aṁ-tāḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> remain in buttocks, genitals <span class="pv-note">— and anus too —</span>, <ruby>and both thighs<rp> (</rp><rt>kaṭi-guhya-ūru-yugma-gāḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. <ruby>Likewise<rp> (</rp><rt>tathā... tathā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>the letters 'e' and 'ai'<rp> (</rp><rt>e-ai-kārau</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(are assigned)</em> <ruby>to the knees<rp> (</rp><rt>jānunī</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. <em>(And)</em> the two next <em>(letters)</em> <span class="pv-note">— i.e. 'o' and 'au' —</span> <span class="pv-w">(tad-parau)</span> <em>(are assigned)</em> to the shanks <span class="pv-note">— right and left, respectively —</span> <span class="pv-w">(jaṅghe)</span>, <em>(while the final two letters,)</em> viz. <ruby>'da' and 'pha'<rp> (</rp><rt>da-phau</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(are assigned)</em> <ruby>to the feet<rp> (</rp><rt>caraṇau</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. <ruby>For this reason<rp> (</rp><rt>atas</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, listen <em>(now)</em> <span class="pv-w">(śṛṇu)</span> <ruby>how<rp> (</rp><rt>yathā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>Vidyā-s<rp> (</rp><rt>vidyāḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> and (ca... ca) <ruby>Mantra-s<rp> (</rp><rt>mantrāḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>are drawn out<rp> (</rp><rt>samuddhāryāḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>!||39-41||</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">Now the aṅganyāsa (the act of placing the hands on different areas of the body while repeating various mantra-s). This practice has as its goal the transformation of the gross physical body into a divine reality by means of assigning sacred sounds to different parts of such physical body. The final result is the promotion of the owner of this physical body to higher states of consciousness. Hence all the effort and time invested in this special practice.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">In this case, the aṅganyāsa will be accompanied by the repetition of the Sanskrit alphabet in its Mālinī arrangement:</p>

<p class="pv-iast"><strong>na ṛ ṝ ḷ ḹ tha ca dha ī ṇa u ū ba ka kha ga gha ṅa i a va bha ya ḍa ḍha ṭha jha ña ja ra ṭa pa cha la ā sa aḥ ha ṣa kṣa ma śa aṁ ta e ai o au da pha</strong></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">This teaching given from the stanza 37 to the first half of the stanza 41 here, in Mālinīvijayottaratantra, is also given by Abhinavagupta in the chapter 15 of his venerable Tantrāloka (from stanza 121 to the first half of the stanza 125). I will write these stanza-s of Tantrāloka right now. Anyway, even with the Abhinavagupta's exposition, it is not enough to understand five confusing things in this aṅganyāsa: The meanings of 'garland of skulls, śūlaśikhā, śūladaṇḍa, kapāla and milk'. To explain these mysterious terms, I have to resort to the commentary that Jayaratha wrote on Abhinavagupta's Tantrāloka. There is no warranty that this topic will be elucidated anyway, but I do not see another way of shedding light on the meanings of these five words. But first the stanzas in Tantrāloka (chapter 15), which are very similar to the ones in Mālinīvijayottaratantra here:</p>

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">न शिखा ऋ ॠ ऌ ॡ च शिरोमाला थ मस्तकम्।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Na śikhā ṛ ṝ ḷ ḹ ca śiromālā tha mastakam|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">नेत्राणि चोर्ध्वे धोऽन्ये ई घ्राणं मुद्रे णु णू श्रुती॥१२१॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Netrāṇi cordhve dho'nye ī ghrāṇaṁ mudre ṇu ṇū śrutī||121||</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">बकवर्गैआ वक्त्रदन्तजिह्वागिरि क्रमात्।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Bakavargaiā vaktradantajihvāgiri kramāt|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">वभया कण्ठदक्षादिस्कन्धयोर्भुजयोर्डढौ॥१२२॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Vabhayā kaṇṭhadakṣādiskandhayorbhujayorḍaḍhau||122||</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">ठो हस्तयोर्झञौ शाखा ज्रटौ शूलकपालके।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Ṭho hastayorjhañau śākhā jraṭau śūlakapālake|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">प हृच्छलौ स्तनौ क्षीरमा स जीवो विसर्गयुक्॥१२३॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Pa hṛcchalau stanau kṣīramā sa jīvo visargayuk||123||</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">प्राणो हवर्णः कथितः षक्षावुदरनाभिगौ।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Prāṇo havarṇaḥ kathitaḥ ṣakṣāvudaranābhigau|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">मशान्ता कटिगुह्योरुयुग्मगा जानुनी तथा॥१२४॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Maśāntā kaṭiguhyoruyugmagā jānunī tathā||124||</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">एऐकारौ तत्परौ तु जङ्घे चरणगौ दफौ।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Eaikārau tatparau tu jaṅghe caraṇagau daphau|</span></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><em>(The letter)</em> <ruby>'Na'<rp> (</rp><rt>na</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(is assigned to)</em> the śikhā <span class="pv-note">— lit. flame —</span> <span class="pv-w">(śikhā)</span> <ruby>and<rp> (</rp><rt>ca</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> 'ṛ', 'ṝ', 'ḷ' <em>(and)</em> <ruby>'ḹ'<rp> (</rp><rt>ṛṝ ḷḹ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(to)</em> the headband <span class="pv-note">— lit. garland of skulls —</span> <span class="pv-w">(śiras-mālā)</span>. <ruby>'Tha'<rp> (</rp><rt>tha</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(is assigned to)</em> the <em>(top of the)</em> <ruby>head<rp> (</rp><rt>mastakam</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. <ruby>And on the forehead<rp> (</rp><rt>ūrdhve</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>on the right and on the left<rp> (</rp><rt>anye</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, 'ca' <em>(and)</em> 'dha' (ca dha) <em>(are assigned to)</em> the eyes <span class="pv-note">— right and left, respectively —</span> <span class="pv-w">(netrāṇi)</span>, <ruby>'ī'<rp> (</rp><rt>ī</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(to)</em> <ruby>the nose<rp> (</rp><rt>ghrāṇam</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(and)</em> <ruby>'ṇa', 'u' and 'ū'<rp> (</rp><rt>ṇu ṇū</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(to)</em> <ruby>the ears<rp> (</rp><rt>śrutī</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>oh You who are endowed with mudrā-s or seals<rp> (</rp><rt>mudre</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">'Ba', the ka-varga <em>(consisting of)</em> 'ka, kha, ga, gha and ṅa' <em>(plus)</em> 'i' <em>(and)</em> <ruby>'a'<rp> (</rp><rt>bakavargaiāḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(are to be positioned)</em> <ruby>in mouth, teeth, tongue and speech<rp> (</rp><rt>vaktra-danta-jihvā-giri</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>in succession<rp> (</rp><rt>kramāt</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. 'Va', 'bha' <em>(and)</em> <ruby>'ya'<rp> (</rp><rt>vabhayāḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(are to be positioned)</em> <ruby>in throat, right shoulder and left shoulder<rp> (</rp><rt>kaṇṭha-dakṣa-ādi-skandhayoḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <em>(while)</em> <ruby>'ḍa' and 'ḍha'<rp> (</rp><rt>ḍaḍhau</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>in the arms<rp> (</rp><rt>bhujayoḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><em>(The letter)</em> <ruby>'ṭha'<rp> (</rp><rt>ṭhaḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(is positioned)</em> <ruby>in both hands<rp> (</rp><rt>hastayoḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>'jha' and 'ña'<rp> (</rp><rt>jha-ñau</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(are assigned)</em> to the fingers <em>(of the right and left hands)</em> <span class="pv-w">(śākhāḥ)</span>, <ruby>'ja', 'ra' and 'ṭa'<rp> (</rp><rt>jraṭau</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(are positioned)</em> <ruby>in śūlaśikhā, śūladaṇḍa and kapāla<rp> (</rp><rt>śūlakapālake</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. 'Pa' <em>(is assigned)</em> <ruby>to the heart<rp> (</rp><rt>pa-hṛd</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>'cha' and 'la'<rp> (</rp><rt>cha-lau</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(are assigned)</em> to both <ruby>portions of the chest --right and left ones--<rp> (</rp><rt>stanau</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>'ā'<rp> (</rp><rt>ā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(is assigned)</em> <ruby>to the milk<rp> (</rp><rt>kṣīram</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>'sa'<rp> (</rp><rt>sa</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(is assigned)</em> <ruby>to the Self<rp> (</rp><rt>jīvaḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <em>(and)</em> visarga --i.e. 'aḥ'-- <em>(is assigned)</em> <ruby>to the general prāṇa<rp> (</rp><rt>visarga-yuk</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. <ruby>The letter 'ha'--<rp> (</rp><rt>ha-varṇaḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>is said to be<rp> (</rp><rt>kathitaḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(assigned to)</em> the <em>(special)</em> <ruby>prāṇa<rp> (</rp><rt>prāṇaḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. <ruby>'Ṣa' and 'kṣa'<rp> (</rp><rt>ṣa-kṣau</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>remain in belly and navel<rp> (</rp><rt>udara-nābhi-gau</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. <ruby>'Ma', 'śa', 'aṁ' and 'ta'<rp> (</rp><rt>ma-śa-aṁ-tāḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> remain in buttocks, <ruby>genitals --and anus too--, and both thighs<rp> (</rp><rt>kaṭi-guhya-ūru-yugma-gāḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. <ruby>Likewise<rp> (</rp><rt>tathā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>the letters 'e' and 'ai'<rp> (</rp><rt>e-ai-kārau</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(are assigned)</em> <ruby>to the knees<rp> (</rp><rt>jānunī</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. <em>(And)</em> the two next <em>(letters)</em> --i.e. <ruby>'o' and 'au'--<rp> (</rp><rt>tad-parau</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(are assigned)</em> <ruby>to the shanks --right and left, respectively--<rp> (</rp><rt>tu jaṅghe</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <em>(while the final two letters,)</em> viz. <ruby>'da' and 'pha'<rp> (</rp><rt>da-phau</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>remain in the feet<rp> (</rp><rt>caraṇa-gau</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>||121-125a||</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">Now, I will analyze the commentary by Jayaratha <ruby>on these stanzas in his Tantrālokaviveka<rp> (</rp><rt>the scholarly commentary on Tantrāloka</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> in order to trace an satisfactory interpretation about the abovementioned five mysterious terms. And in the process, I will explain other things too:</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><ruby>Regarding the first mysterious word<rp> (</rp><rt>'śiromālā', lit. garland of skulls</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, Jayaratha specifies this: 'शिरोमाला कपालारम्भिका।' - Śiromālā kapālārambhikā|<ruby>' - 'The garland of skulls<rp> (</rp><rt>śiras-mālā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(is)</em> <ruby>what holds skulls<rp> (</rp><rt>kapāla-ārambhikā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>|'. Well, this adds nothing really. My translation as 'headband', i.e. as the area of the head covered by a headband looks more useful here.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">With respect to the next three mysterious words, 'śūlaśikhā, śūladaṇḍa, kapāla', contained in the expression 'śūlakapālake' in the stanza 39 of Mālinīvijayottaratantra, Jayaratha specifies this: 'ज्रेति दण्डेन सह शूलं तेन शूलस्य दण्डे रेफः शिखासु जकारः। स च उत्तानो येन साक्षात्शूलत्वं स्यात्।' - 'Jreti daṇḍena saha śūlaṁ tena śūlasya daṇḍe rephaḥ śikhāsu jakāraḥ| Sa ca uttāno yena sākṣātśūlatvaṁ syāt|' - '<em>(The letters)</em> ja and ra (jra iti) <em>(are)</em> <ruby>the trident<rp> (</rp><rt>śūlam</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>together with the shaft<rp> (</rp><rt>daṇḍena saha</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. <ruby>Therefore<rp> (</rp><rt>tena</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>in the shaft<rp> (</rp><rt>daṇḍe</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>of the trident<rp> (</rp><rt>śūlasya</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> the letter ra <em>(remains)</em> <span class="pv-w">(rephaḥ)</span> <em>(and)</em> in <em>(its)</em> <ruby>prongs<rp> (</rp><rt>śikhāsu</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> the letter ja <em>(remains)</em> <span class="pv-w">(ja-kāraḥ)</span>. By means of placing the <em>(right hand)</em> <ruby>turned upward<rp> (</rp><rt>saḥ ca uttānaḥ yena</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>the form of the trident<rp> (</rp><rt>śūlatvam</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>appears<rp> (</rp><rt>syāt</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>before one's very eyes<rp> (</rp><rt>sākṣāt</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>|'.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">Next Jayaratha quotes a stanza from Siddhātantra, in which the trident and its shaft are assigned to the right hand (hence I translated like this above). <ruby>Nothing is said about the 'kapāla'<rp> (</rp><rt>skull</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> and the letter 'ṭa'. But Jayaratha quotes later a stanza of venerable Triśirobhairava, in which it is clearly stated that the letter 'ṭa' is in the left hand. Really, Jayaratha did not expand a lot about this topic of the trident and the skull in the right and left hands.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">With reference to the last mysterious word, 'milk', which is connected with the letter 'ā', Jayaratha is not commenting anything at all. Nevertheless, Abhinavagupta, in other portions of his Tantrāloka, considers 'milk' to be 'Amṛta' or the Nectar of Immortality, i.e. the Highest Consciousness.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">Very well, these five mysterious words are now elucidated to a certain extent. But there is still the explanation of certain things: I translated "... <ruby>'sa'<rp> (</rp><rt>sa</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(is assigned)</em> <ruby>to the Self<rp> (</rp><rt>jīvaḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <em>(and)</em> visarga <span class="pv-note">— i.e. 'aḥ' —</span> <em>(is assigned)</em> <ruby>to the general prāṇa<rp> (</rp><rt>visarga-yuk</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. The next one <span class="pv-note">— i.e. 'ha' —</span> <span class="pv-w">(tatparaḥ)</span> <ruby>is said to be<rp> (</rp><rt>kathitaḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(assigned to)</em> the <em>(special)</em> <ruby>prāṇa<rp> (</rp><rt>prāṇaḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>...". In this I was following the commentary by Jayaratha to the letter. See: 'जीव इति आत्मा सामान्यप्रानात्मा च। तयोश्च अभेदादेकतया निर्देशस्तेन साक्षादात्मनि सकारः प्राणात्मनि तु विसर्गः। प्राण इति विशिष्टः।' - 'Jīva iti ātmā sāmānyaprānātmā ca| Tayośca abhedādekatayā nirdeśastena sākṣādātmani sakāraḥ prāṇātmani tu visargaḥ| Prāṇa iti viśiṣṭaḥ|<ruby>' - "'Jīva'<rp> (</rp><rt>jīvaḥ iti</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(is connected with)</em> <ruby>Self<rp> (</rp><rt>ātmā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>as well as<rp> (</rp><rt>ca</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(with)</em> <ruby>general prāṇa<rp> (</rp><rt>sāmānya-prāna-ātmā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>| Due to <em>(their)</em> <ruby>close union<rp> (</rp><rt>abhedāt</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <em>(there is)</em> <ruby>indication<rp> (</rp><rt>nirdeśaḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> of those two <span class="pv-note">— viz. of Self and general prāṇa —</span> <span class="pv-w">(tayoḥ ca)</span> <ruby>as being one<rp> (</rp><rt>ekatayā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <span class="pv-note">— i.e. Self and general prāṇa, because of their close union, are indicated as being one and the same thing —</span>. <ruby>For that reason<rp> (</rp><rt>tena</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>the letter 'sa'<rp> (</rp><rt>sa-kāraḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(is positioned)</em> <ruby>in the Self in person<rp> (</rp><rt>sākṣāt ātmani</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>while<rp> (</rp><rt>tu</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> visarga <span class="pv-note">— i.e. 'aḥ' —</span> <span class="pv-w">(visargaḥ)</span> <em>(is positioned)</em> in the <em>(general)</em> <ruby>prāṇa<rp> (</rp><rt>prāṇa-ātmani</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>| <em>(And)</em> the letter 'ha' <span class="pv-note">— called 'prāṇa' here —</span> <span class="pv-w">(prāṇaḥ iti)</span> <em>(represents)</em> special <em>(prāṇa)</em> <span class="pv-w">(viśiṣṭaḥ)</span>|". Now it is very clear!</p>

## Строфы 42–50 {#s5}

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="s5-sa" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">सबिन्दुकां दक्षजङ्घां ततो वाचं प्रकल्पयेत्।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Sabindukāṁ dakṣajaṅghāṁ tato vācaṁ prakalpayet|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">तथैव जङ्घया युक्तं चतुर्थं दशनं ततः॥४२॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Tathaiva jaṅghayā yuktaṁ caturthaṁ daśanaṁ tataḥ||42||</span></p>

<p class="pv-en pv-anchor" id="s5-ru" lang="en"><ruby>One should arrange<rp> (</rp><rt>prakalpayet</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(the letter assigned to)</em> the right shank <span class="pv-note">— i.e. 'o' —</span> <span class="pv-w">(dakṣa-jaṅghām)</span> —along with bindu <span class="pv-note">— i.e. 'ṁ' —</span> <span class="pv-w">(sa-bindukām)</span>—, <em>(and)</em> <ruby>after that<rp> (</rp><rt>tatas</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(one should add the letter assigned to)</em> the speech <span class="pv-note">— i.e. 'a' —</span> <span class="pv-w">(vācam)</span>. <ruby>In the same way<rp> (</rp><rt>tathā eva</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <em>(one should add)</em> <ruby>afterward<rp> (</rp><rt>tatas</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(the letter for)</em> the fourth tooth <span class="pv-note">— i.e. 'gha' —</span> <span class="pv-w">(caturtham daśanam)</span> <ruby>joined with<rp> (</rp><rt>yuktam</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(the letter for)</em> the <em>(right)</em> shank <span class="pv-note">— i.e. 'o' —</span> <span class="pv-w">(jaṅghayā)</span> <span class="pv-note">— all in all, one has so far formed this portion of the mantra: 'Oṁ agho' —</span>||42||</p>

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="v50" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">ततः परमघोरान्तं पाद्यकाद्ये च पूर्ववत्।</span><br />

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><ruby>After that<rp> (</rp><rt>tatas</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <em>(one should add the bījamantra-s)</em> which are at the end of 'paramaghore' <em>(in this mantra)</em> <span class="pv-note">— i.e. 'huṁ' and 'haḥ' —</span> <span class="pv-w">(paramaghora-antam)</span>. <em>(Next, one should add)</em> the letters 'pha' and 'ṭa' <span class="pv-note">— i.e. 'phaṭ' —</span> <span class="pv-w">(pādya-kādye ca)</span> <ruby>as before<rp> (</rp><rt>pūrva-vat</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. <em>(Thus, it has been)</em> <ruby>proclaimed<rp> (</rp><rt>samākhyātā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>the Parāparā's mantra<rp> (</rp><rt>parā-aparā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <span class="pv-note">— viz. the mantra 'Oṁ aghore hrīḥ paramaghore huṁ ghorarūpe haḥ ghoramukhi bhīma bhīṣaṇe vama piba he ruru rara phaṭ huṁ haḥ phaṭ' —</span>. <ruby>And<rp> (</rp><rt>ca</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(now)</em> <ruby>the Aparā's mantra<rp> (</rp><rt>aparā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>is announced<rp> (</rp><rt>prakathyate</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>||50||</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">What Śiva did is the following: He encoded the mantra of the Parāparā <span class="pv-w">(the goddess Parāparā who is the aspect of Śakti generating unity in diversity)</span> by mentioning the different realities associated with Sanskrit letters instead of 'directly' mentioning the letters themselves. But before continuing with my analysis, what is the Parāparā goddess really? This goddess <ruby>is one of the three main aspects of the Goddess<rp> (</rp><rt>Śakti, the Power of Śiva</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">At first, this Śakti remains only as Icchāśakti (Power of Will) in Śaktitattva (category 2). She is only Will to manifest, but there is no plan yet, let alone an actual manifestation. <ruby>This first aspect of Hers is called Parā<rp> (</rp><rt>Supreme</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. The only Knowledge here is Knowledge about Śiva Himself. There is no universe being known at all. Another name for this pure Will of Śiva is Svātantrya or Absolute Freedom. Freedom for what? Freedom to manifest whatever thing at Will.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">Later, in order to perform the development of the 'universal plan', Jñānaśakti (Power of Knowledge) appears and mixes with Icchāśakti (Power of Will). <ruby>This is the category 3<rp> (</rp><rt>Sadāśivatattva</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. This mixture of <ruby>Will and Knowledge is the second aspect of Hers<rp> (</rp><rt>of Śakti</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. <ruby>She is then called Parāparā<rp> (</rp><rt>Supreme-nonSupreme</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. Here Knowledge is about Śiva as well as about the universe who has been determined or planned. There is Will to manifest and a determination or plan too carried out by the Power of Knowledge. That is why Śiva defined Jñānaśakti before (in the stanza 6 of this chapter) like this: "By announcing with determination that 'This knowable or object is like this and not otherwise!'". All in all, Jñānaśakti determines or plans the universe.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">Finally, in order to actualize the aforesaid determination or plan regarding the universe, Kriyāśakti (Power of Action) appears and mixes with Icchāśakti and Jñānaśakti. <ruby>This is the category 4<rp> (</rp><rt>Īśvaratattva</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. This mixture of Will, Knowledge <ruby>and Action is the third aspect of Hers<rp> (</rp><rt>of Śakti</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. <ruby>She is then called Aparā<rp> (</rp><rt>nonSupreme</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. Here Knowledge is only about the universe who has been produced. There is Will to manifest, a determination or <ruby>plan plus an actual production of such universe<rp> (</rp><rt>i.e. Icchāśakti, Jñānaśakti and Kriyāśakti</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. That is why Śiva defined Kriyāśakti before (in the stanza 7 of this chapter) like this:"'Let this thing become so!'". All in all, Kriyāśakti actually brings about the universe.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">Now I will explain this Parāparā's mantra. First I will put the mantra both in Devanāgarī and IAST:</p>

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">ॐ अघोरे ह्रीः परमघोरे हुं घोररूपे हः घोरमुखि भीम भीषणे वम पिब हे रुरु रर फट् हुं हः फट्</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Oṁ aghore hrīḥ paramaghore huṁ ghorarūpe haḥ ghoramukhi bhīma bhīṣaṇe vama piba he ruru rara phaṭ huṁ haḥ phaṭ</span></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><ruby>'Oṁ<rp> (</rp><rt>oṁ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, oh Aghorā (aghore... he), <ruby>hrīḥ<rp> (</rp><rt>hrīḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, oh Paramaghorā (paramaghore... he), <ruby>huṁ<rp> (</rp><rt>huṁ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>oh Ghorarūpā<rp> (</rp><rt>ghorarūpe... he</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>haḥ<rp> (</rp><rt>haḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, oh Ghoramukhī (ghoramukhi... he), <ruby>oh Bhīmā<rp> (</rp><rt>bhīma... he</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>oh Bhīṣaṇā<rp> (</rp><rt>bhīṣaṇe... he</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, oh Vamanī (vama... he), oh Pibanī (piba... he), <ruby>ruru rara phaṭ huṁ haḥ phaṭ<rp> (</rp><rt>ruru rara phaṭ huṁ haḥ phaṭ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>'</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">The mantra contains 39 syllables. Apart from bījamantra-s (one-syllabic mantra-s), it contains the Vocative cases of eight deities: Aghorā, Paramaghorā, Ghorarūpā, Ghoramukhī, Bhīmā, Bhīṣaṇā, Vamanī and Pibanī. So, if you have some knowledge about Sanskrit declension, you would say that the Vocative cases of those eight words are like this: Aghore, Paramaghore, Ghorarūpe, Ghoramukhi, Bhīme, Bhīṣaṇe, Vamani and Pibani. Anyway, you read in the mantra: Aghore, Paramaghore, Ghorarūpe, Ghoramukhi, Bhīma, Bhīṣaṇe, Vama and Piba. What happened here? Abhinavagupta explains very well why 'Bhīme, Vamani and Pibani' were written by Śiva as 'Bhīma, Vama and Piba'. He does so in the chapter 30 (stanzas 21 and 22) of his famous Tantrāloka. After this, Abhinavagupta explains more things about this mantra. Also, this mantra is used to purify (to make a person conscious of) the 36 tattva-s by assigning the different words to specific tattva-s. This is again explained by Abhinavagupta in the chapter 16 (from stanza 213 up to stanza 216).</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">Besides, the <ruby>first portion, from 'Oṁ' up to 'he', is Śākta<rp> (</rp><rt>pertains to Śakti</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> while the second portion, from 'ru ru' right to the end, i.e. <ruby>right to the final 'phaṭ', is Śaiva<rp> (</rp><rt>pertains to Śiva</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. This Parāparāmantra is used in rituals related to the deities of the Parāparā aspect of the Goddess. <ruby>The eight deities invoked<rp> (</rp><rt>Aghorā, Paramaghorā, etc.</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> are the śakti-s of the eight Vijñānākala-s awakened by Śiva, which are mentioned in the stanza 21 of the first chapter of this Mālinīvijayottaratantra. Other names for these 8 deities are: <ruby>Māheśī<rp> (</rp><rt>or Māheśvarī</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>Brāhmaṇī<rp> (</rp><rt>or Brāhmī</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>Skāndī<rp> (</rp><rt>Kaumārī</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>Vaiṣṇavī, Aindrī<rp> (</rp><rt>or Indrāṇī</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>Yāmyā<rp> (</rp><rt>or Vārāhī</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>Cāmuṇḍā and Yogīśī<rp> (</rp><rt>or Yogīśvarī or Mahālakṣmī</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, who are the abovementioned deities of the varga-s or groups of letters (see stanza 14 in this chapter). OK, it is enough for now.</p>

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">ह्रीः हूं फट्</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Hrīḥ hūṁ phaṭ</span></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">No meaning. Just three bījamantra-s.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">The mantra contains 3.5 syllables as phaṭ contains 1.<ruby>5 syllables<rp> (</rp><rt>pha + ṭ = 1 + 0.5</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. This Aparā mantra is used in rituals related to the deities of the Aparā aspect of the Goddess.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">Short explanation but meaningful.</p>

<hr class="pv-rule" />

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="v52" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">अपरेयं समाख्याता रुद्रशक्तिं परां शृणु।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Apareyaṁ samākhyātā rudraśaktiṁ parāṁ śṛṇu|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">मन्त्राः संमुखतां यान्ति ययोच्चारितमात्रया॥५२॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Mantrāḥ saṁmukhatāṁ yānti yayoccāritamātrayā||52||</span></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><ruby>This<rp> (</rp><rt>iyam</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>Aparā's mantra<rp> (</rp><rt>aparā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> has been <em>(so)</em> proclaimed <em>(in the previous stanza)</em> <span class="pv-w">(samākhyātā)</span>. <em>(Now)</em> <ruby>listen to<rp> (</rp><rt>śṛṇu</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>the Parā's mantra<rp> (</rp><rt>parām</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> who is the Power of Rudra <span class="pv-note">— viz. Śiva —</span> <span class="pv-w">(rudra-śaktim)</span>, <ruby>by merely pronouncing which<rp> (</rp><rt>yayā uccārita-mātrayā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>the mantra-s<rp> (</rp><rt>mantrāḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>become propitious<rp> (</rp><rt>saṁmukhatām yānti</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>||52||</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">Here Śiva simply announces that the Aparā's mantra has been taught in the stanza 51 and that the Parā's mantra is about to be proclaimed in the upcoming stanzas. Anyway, He said something very important about the Parā's mantra: That the rest of mantra-s become propitious when combined with the Parā's mantra. That is why the Parā's mantra is abundantly used in conjunction with other mantra-s during rituals and invocations.</p>

<hr class="pv-rule" />

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="v53" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">कम्पते गात्रयष्टिश्च द्रुतं चोत्पतनं भवेत्।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Kampate gātrayaṣṭiśca drutaṁ cotpatanaṁ bhavet|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">मुद्राबन्धं च गेयं च शिवारुदितमेव च॥५३॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Mudrābandhaṁ ca geyaṁ ca śivāruditameva ca||53||</span></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">The slender body <em>(of the knower of Parā's mantra)</em> <span class="pv-w">(gātrayaṣṭiḥ)</span> <ruby>trembles<rp> (</rp><rt>kampate</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> and (ca... ca) <ruby>quickly<rp> (</rp><rt>drutam</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> rises up (utpatanam bhavet), assumes <em>(various)</em> <ruby>mudrā-s and bandha-s<rp> (</rp><rt>mudrā-bandham</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>sings<rp> (</rp><rt>geyam</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> and (ca... ca... eva ca) <ruby>howls like a jackal<rp> (</rp><rt>śivā-ruditam</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>||53||</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">Śiva just describes what happens to the great Yogī who is perfectly aware of this mantra, i.e. of its meaning and of its being rooted in Aham or I-consciousness full of Absolute Freedom.</p>

<hr class="pv-rule" />

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="v54" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">अतीतानागतार्थस्य कुर्याद्वा कथनादिकम्।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Atītānāgatārthasya kuryādvā kathanādikam|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">वामजङ्घान्वितो जीवः पारम्पर्यक्रमागतः॥५४॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Vāmajaṅghānvito jīvaḥ pāramparyakramāgataḥ||54||</span></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><em>(And a person like this)</em> <ruby>can give information, etc.<rp> (</rp><rt>kuryāt... kathana-ādikam</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>about past or future things<rp> (</rp><rt>atīta-anāgata-arthasya... vā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. <em>(The letter associated with)</em> the individual soul <span class="pv-note">— i.e. 'sa' —</span> <span class="pv-w">(jīvaḥ)</span> accompanied by <em>(the letter for)</em> the left shank <span class="pv-note">— i.e. 'au' —</span> <span class="pv-w">(vāma-jaṅghā-anvitaḥ)</span> has come through an uninterrupted succession <em>(of Guru-s and disciples)</em> <span class="pv-w">(pāramparya-krama-āgataḥ)</span>||54||</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">A Yogī like this can inform about the past or the future.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">What Śiva did is the following: <ruby>He encoded the mantra of the Parā<rp> (</rp><rt>the goddess Parā who is the aspect of Śakti generating unity</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> by mentioning the different realities associated with Sanskrit letters instead of 'directly' mentioning the letters themselves. This goddess <ruby>is one of the three main aspects of the Goddess<rp> (</rp><rt>Śakti, the Power of Śiva</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. These aspects were already described by me under the 50th stanza.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">Now I will explain this Parā's mantra. First I will put the mantra both in Devanāgarī and IAST:</p>

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">सौः</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Sauḥ</span></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">No meaning.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">The mantra contains 1 syllable. This Parāmantra is used in rituals related to the Parā aspect of the Goddess. The letter 'sa' covers all the 31 tattva-s from Pṛthivītattva (category 36) up to Māyātattva (category 6). The <ruby>letter 'au' covers the categories 5, 4 and 3<rp> (</rp><rt>Sadvidyātattva, Īśvaratattva and Sadāśivatattva</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. <ruby>Finally, the visarga 'ḥ'<rp> (</rp><rt>which is not directly mentioned by Śiva</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> covers the first two categories: Śivatattva and Śaktitattva. This supreme mantra has been very well explained by Kṣemarāja in his Parāprāveśikā.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">Another short but meaningful explanation.</p>

<hr class="pv-rule" />

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="v55" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">परेयमनया सिद्धिः सर्वकामफलप्रदा।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Pareyamanayā siddhiḥ sarvakāmaphalapradā|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">नाशिष्याय प्रदेयेयं नाभक्ताय कदाचन॥५५॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Nāśiṣyāya pradeyeyaṁ nābhaktāya kadācana||55||</span></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><em>(So,)</em> <ruby>this<rp> (</rp><rt>iyam</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(is)</em> the Parā_<ruby>'s<rp> (</rp><rt>mantra</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>_ <span class="pv-w">(parā)</span>. <ruby>By Her<rp> (</rp><rt>anayā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(there is)</em> a Siddhi or Accomplishment <span class="pv-note">— viz. Liberation —</span> <span class="pv-w">(siddhiḥ)</span> <ruby>which grants the fruits of all the desires<rp> (</rp><rt>sarva-kāma-phala-pradā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. This <em>(Parā's mantra)</em> <span class="pv-w">(iyam)</span> <ruby>should never be given<rp> (</rp><rt>na... pradeyā... na... kadācana</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>to someone who is not a disciple<rp> (</rp><rt>aśiṣyāya</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(or)</em> <ruby>a devotee<rp> (</rp><rt>abhaktāya</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>||55||</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">This Great Mantra is like cintāmaṇi (the jewel which yields all desires) indeed. And, this is very important, the Parā's mantra must never be given to someone who is not a disciple or a devotee. I would add that this holds true for all the spiritual teachings in general, not only this mantra.</p>

<hr class="pv-rule" />

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="v56" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">रुद्रश्च रुद्रशक्तिश्च गुरुश्चेति त्रयं समम्।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Rudraśca rudraśaktiśca guruśceti trayaṁ samam|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">भक्त्या प्रपश्यते यस्तु तस्मै देया वरानने॥५६॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Bhaktyā prapaśyate yastu tasmai deyā varānane||56||</span></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><ruby>Oh lovely-faced One<rp> (</rp><rt>vara-ānane</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <em>(this Parā's mantra)</em> <ruby>should be certainly given<rp> (</rp><rt>tu... deyā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>to the one<rp> (</rp><rt>tasmai</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>who<rp> (</rp><rt>yaḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>through devotion<rp> (</rp><rt>bhaktyā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>perceives<rp> (</rp><rt>prapaśyate</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>in like manner<rp> (</rp><rt>samam</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(this)</em> <ruby>group of three<rp> (</rp><rt>muestra</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>: 'Rudra <span class="pv-note">— Śiva —</span> <span class="pv-w">(rudraḥ)</span>, the Power of Rudra <span class="pv-note">— Śakti —</span> <span class="pv-w">(rudra-śaktiḥ)</span> and (ca... ca.. ca) <ruby>Guru<rp> (</rp><rt>guruḥ... iti</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>'||56||</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">The one receiving the Parā's mantra must consider Śiva, Śakti and Guru to be one Reality. If someone fails to do this, he should never receive this Great Mantra till he develops this attitude of triple unity.</p>

<hr class="pv-rule" />

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="v57" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">शिष्येणापि तदा ग्राह्या यदा सन्तोषितो गुरुः।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Śiṣyeṇāpi tadā grāhyā yadā santoṣito guruḥ|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">शरीरद्रव्यविज्ञानशुद्धिकर्मगुणादिभिः॥५७॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Śarīradravyavijñānaśuddhikarmaguṇādibhiḥ||57||</span></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><ruby>When<rp> (</rp><rt>yadā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>the Guru<rp> (</rp><rt>guruḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>has been pleased<rp> (</rp><rt>santoṣitaḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>by the disciple<rp> (</rp><rt>śiṣyeṇa api</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> through <em>(his)</em> body, substances, purification of <ruby>his understanding, actions and good qualities<rp> (</rp><rt>śarīra-dravya-vijñāna-śuddhi-karma-guṇādibhiḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>then<rp> (</rp><rt>tadā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(the aforesaid Parā's mantra)</em> <ruby>can be received<rp> (</rp><rt>grāhyā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> by him <span class="pv-note">— lit. by the disciple —</span> <span class="pv-w">(śiṣyeṇa api)</span>||57||</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">The disciple can receive the Parā's mantra only when he has pleased his Guru by using his body (i.e. by using his body to serve his Guru), substances (i.e. by using different substances during his worship of his Guru, e.g. incense, camphor, ghee, etc.), purification of his understanding <span class="pv-w">(i.e. by using his new understanding forged by the hard study of Śiva's scriptures)</span>, purification of his actions (i.e. by using his actions which are now carried out while there is at the same time a focus on the unity of all the things), good qualities (i.e. by using the good qualities he received from the Great Lord such acceptance, faith, patience, etc.).</p>

<hr class="pv-rule" />

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="v58" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">बोधिता तु यदा तेन गुरुणा हृष्टचेतसा।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Bodhitā tu yadā tena guruṇā hṛṣṭacetasā|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">तदा सिद्धिप्रदा ज्ञेया नान्यथा वीरवन्दिते॥५८॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Tadā siddhipradā jñeyā nānyathā vīravandite||58||</span></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><ruby>Oh You who are extolled by the Heroes<rp> (</rp><rt>vīra-vandite</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>when<rp> (</rp><rt>yadā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> She <span class="pv-note">— the Parā's mantra —</span> <ruby>is made known<rp> (</rp><rt>bodhitā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>indeed<rp> (</rp><rt>tu</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>by that Guru who is very pleased<rp> (</rp><rt>tena guruṇā hṛṣṭa-cetasā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>then<rp> (</rp><rt>tadā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>She is known<rp> (</rp><rt>jñeyā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> as the Siddhi-bestower <span class="pv-note">— as the One giving Accomplishment, viz. Liberation —</span> <span class="pv-w">(siddhi-pradā)</span> <em>(and)</em> <ruby>not otherwise<rp> (</rp><rt>na anyathā</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>||58||</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">When the Guru is that pleased with the diligent good disciple, then he will give the disciple this Parā's mantra which will bestow on the latter the fruit known as Liberation from Saṁsāra (transmigration replete with misery).</p>

<hr class="pv-rule" />

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="v59" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">परापराङ्गसम्भूता योगिन्योऽष्टौ महाबलाः।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Parāparāṅgasambhūtā yoginyo'ṣṭau mahābalāḥ|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">पञ्च षट् पञ्च चत्वारि द्वित्रिद्व्यर्णाः क्रमेण तु॥५९॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Pañca ṣaṭ pañca catvāri dvitridvyarṇāḥ krameṇa tu||59||</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">ज्ञेयाः सप्तैकादशार्णा एकार्धार्णद्वयान्विताः।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Jñeyāḥ saptaikādaśārṇā ekārdhārṇadvayānvitāḥ|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">जीवो दीर्घस्वरैः षड्भिः पृथग्जातिसमन्वितः॥६०॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Jīvo dīrghasvaraiḥ ṣaḍbhiḥ pṛthagjātisamanvitaḥ||60||</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">विद्यात्रयस्य गात्राणि ह्रस्वैर्वक्त्राणि पञ्चभिः।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Vidyātrayasya gātrāṇi hrasvairvaktrāṇi pañcabhiḥ|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">ओङ्कारैः पञ्चभिर्मन्त्रो विद्याङ्गहृदयं भवेत्॥६१॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Oṅkāraiḥ pañcabhirmantro vidyāṅgahṛdayaṁ bhavet||61||</span></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><ruby>The very mighty<rp> (</rp><rt>mahābalāḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>Yoginī-s<rp> (</rp><rt>yoginyaḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>who have become<rp> (</rp><rt>sambhūtāḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>the limbs<rp> (</rp><rt>aṅga</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> of the Parāparā_(mantra)_ <span class="pv-note">— i.e. of 'Oṁ aghore hrīḥ paramaghore huṁ ghorarūpe haḥ ghoramukhi bhīma bhīṣaṇe vama piba he ruru rara phaṭ huṁ haḥ phaṭ', but only the 'Oṁ aghore hrīḥ paramaghore huṁ ghorarūpe haḥ ghoramukhi bhīma bhīṣaṇe vama piba he' portion is relevant here, because the remaining 'ruru rara phaṭ huṁ haḥ phaṭ' portion pertains to Śiva —</span> <span class="pv-w">(parā-aparā)</span> <ruby>are eight<rp> (</rp><rt>aṣṭau</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <span class="pv-note">— viz. Brāhmī, Māheśvarī, Kaumārī, Vaiṣṇavī, Vārāhī, Indrāṇī, Cāmuṇḍā and Yogīśvarī —</span>. <em>(The sacred words assigned to them in the Parāparāmantra contain)</em> <ruby>in succession<rp> (</rp><rt>krameṇa tu</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> five <em>(letters)</em> <span class="pv-note">— i.e. 'Oṁ a-gho-re hrīḥ', for Brāhmī —</span> <span class="pv-w">(pañca)</span>, six <em>(letters)</em> <span class="pv-note">— i.e. 'pa-ra-ma-gho-re huṁ', for Māheśvarī —</span> <span class="pv-w">(ṣaṭ)</span>, five <em>(letters)</em> <span class="pv-note">— i.e. 'gho-ra-rū-pe haḥ', for Kaumārī —</span> <span class="pv-w">(pañca)</span>, four <em>(letters)</em> <span class="pv-note">— i.e. 'gho-ra-mu-khi', for Vaiṣṇavī —</span> <span class="pv-w">(catvāri)</span>, <em>(along with a sequence)</em> <ruby>of two and three<rp> (</rp><rt>dvi-tri</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>letters<rp> (</rp><rt>arṇāḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(occurring)</em> <ruby>twice<rp> (</rp><rt>dvi</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <span class="pv-note">— i.e. 'bhī-ma', for Vārāhī; 'bhī-ṣa-ṇe', for Indrāṇī; 'va-ma', for Cāmuṇḍā; and 'pi-ba he', for Yogīśvarī, respectively —</span>. <em>(Besides,)</em> <ruby>they should be known<rp> (</rp><rt>jñeyāḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> as <em>(constituted by the vocative cases containing the sum of)</em> <ruby>seven and eleven letters<rp> (</rp><rt>sapta-ekādaśa-arṇāḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>accompanied by<rp> (</rp><rt>anvitāḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> one <em>(and)</em> <ruby>one and a half letters<rp> (</rp><rt>ardha-arṇa</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> twice <span class="pv-note">— viz. one and a half letters 'twice', i.e. three letters —</span> <span class="pv-w">(dvaya)</span> <span class="pv-note">— the final number is 22 letters in all, which refers to the sum of letters belonging to the following vocative cases (plus the vocative particle 'he' = 'oh!') occurring in the abovementioned Parāparāmantra: a-gho-re, pa-ra-ma-gho-re, gho-ra-rū-pe, gho-ra-mu-khi, bhī-ma, bhī-ṣa-ṇe and he —</span>.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><em>(The letter for)</em> the Self <span class="pv-note">— i.e. 'sa' —</span> <span class="pv-w">(jīvaḥ)</span> together with the group of six long vowels <span class="pv-note">— i.e. 'ā, ī, ū, ai and au' plus 'ṁ', along with 'aḥ' —</span> <span class="pv-w">(dīrgha-svaraiḥ ṣaḍbhiḥ)</span> <em>(arranged)</em> separately <span class="pv-note">— sāṁ, sīṁ, sūṁ, saiṁ, sauṁ and saḥ being used separately —</span> <span class="pv-w">(pṛthak)</span>, <em>(and)</em> accompanied by adverbs of the jāti class <span class="pv-note">— e.g. svāhā, vaṣaṭ, vauṣaṭ, huṁ, namaḥ or phaṭ —</span> <span class="pv-w">(jāti-samanvitaḥ)</span> <em>(are)</em> <ruby>the limbs<rp> (</rp><rt>gātrāṇi</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>of the three Vidyā-s<rp> (</rp><rt>vidyā-trayasya</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. <em>(And when the letter 'sa')</em> is furnished with the five short vowels <span class="pv-note">— i.e. 'a, i, u, e and o' plus 'ṁ' —</span> <span class="pv-w">(hrasvaiḥ... pañcabhiḥ)</span>, <em>(the final result, viz. 'saṁ, siṁ, suṁ, seṁ and soṁ' are)</em> the mouths <em>(of those three Vidyā-s)</em> <span class="pv-w">(vaktrāṇi)</span>. <em>(Finally,)</em> the mantra_(-s)_ <span class="pv-w">(mantraḥ)</span> <ruby>endowed with five Oṁ<rp> (</rp><rt>oṁ-kāraiḥ pañcabhiḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> become <span class="pv-note">— lit. becomes —</span> <span class="pv-w">(bhavet)</span> <ruby>the heart in the bodies of the Vidyā-s<rp> (</rp><rt>vidyā-aṅga-hṛdayam</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>||59-61||</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">Next, Śiva mentioned the vocative cases (including 'he' whose meaning is 'oh!') of the goddesses of the Parāparā's mantra (with the exception of 'vama' and 'piba'). So, here I will put the names of these goddesses and the respective numbers of letters/syllables, which totals 22 letters/syllables as specified by Śiva:</p>

1. Aghore ---> A-gho-re ---> (3 letters/syllables)
2. Paramaghore ---> Pa-ra-ma-gho-re ---> (5 letters/syllables)
3. Ghorarūpe ---> Gho-ra-rū-pe ---> (4 letters/syllables)
4. Ghoramukhi ---> Gho-ra-mu-khi ---> (4 letters/ syllables)
5. Bhīma ---> Bhī-ma ---> (2 letters/syllables)
6. Bhīṣaṇe ---> Bhī-ṣa-ṇe ---> (3 letters/syllables)
7. He ---> He ---> (1 letter/syllable)

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">Next, Śiva joined <ruby>the letter 'sa' with the group of long vowels<rp> (</rp><rt>ā, ī, ū, ai and au plus ṁ, along with aḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, so creating the sounds: sāṁ, sīṁ, sūṁ, saiṁ, sauṁ, saḥ. And according to His teachings, they should <ruby>be used together with adverbs of the jāti class<rp> (</rp><rt>e.g. svāhā, vaṣaṭ, vauṣaṭ, huṁ, namaḥ or phaṭ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. These sounds are used in the rituals, obviously, as they constitute the limbs of the three Vidyā-s, i.e. the Parā, Parāparā and Aparā goddesses. As during the ritual one tries to get fully identification with all these higher realities, he should consider all these mantra-s to be the limbs of his own body. Additionally, when the same letter 'sa' is endowed with the five short vowels <span class="pv-w">(a, i, u, e and o plus ṁ - and, though formally speaking, 'e' and 'o' are long vowels, in this context they must be considered to be short)</span>, <ruby>the result of this combination<rp> (</rp><rt>viz. saṁ, siṁ, suṁ, seṁ, soṁ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> is the mouths of such goddesses, i.e. one's own mouth. All this is completely explained by Abhinavagupta in the chapter 30 of his Tantrāloka.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">At the end of the stanza 61, Śiva announces the mantra-s furnished with five 'Oṁ'. Anyway, He will mention only four in the next stanzas. OK, this elucidation has been more than enough, I guess.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">Abhinavagupta explains this mantra in the stanza 38 inside the chapter 30 of his Tantrāloka.</p>

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="v63" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">वेदवेदिनि हूँफट् च प्रणवादिसमन्विता।</span><br />

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">This mantra is described by Abhinavagupta in the first half of the stanza 39 in the chapter 30 of his Tantrāloka. In my opinion, the length really is eight syllables and a half and not only eight, as phaṭ contains 1.5 syllables.</p>

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="v64" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">वज्रिणे वज्रधराय स्वाहान्तं प्रणवादिकम्।</span><br />

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><strong><em>(3)</em></strong> <em>(This)</em> eleven-syllabic <em>(mantra)</em> <span class="pv-w">(ekādaśa-akṣaram)</span> endowed with Praṇava <span class="pv-note">— Oṁ —</span> <ruby>at the beginning and with 'svāhā' at the end<rp> (</rp><rt>svāhā-antaṁ praṇava-ādikam</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>is said to be<rp> (</rp><rt>smṛtam</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> 'Varma puruṣṭutam' <span class="pv-note">— lit. highly lauded armor <em>(mantra)</em> —</span> <span class="pv-w">(varma puruṣṭutam iti)</span>: '<em>(Oṁ)</em> vajriṇe vajradharāya <em>(svāhā)</em>' <span class="pv-w">(vajriṇe vajra-dharāya)</span>||64||</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">This mantra is described by Abhinavagupta in the second half of the stanza 39 in the chapter 30 of his Tantrāloka.</p>

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="v65" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">श्लीपदं पशुशब्दं च हूँफडन्तं भवादिकम्।</span><br />

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">This mantra is described by Abhinavagupta in the second half of the stanza 41 in the chapter 30 of his Tantrāloka. Anyway, the fifth mantra furnished with 'Oṁ' is absent here, in Mālinīvijayottaratantra (at least in the version I am using). However, Abhinavagupta mentions this missing fifth mantra called 'Netram' in the second half of the stanza 40 and the first half of the stanza 41 in the chapter 30 of his Tantrāloka. It looks like this: 'Oṁ juṁ saḥ'.</p>

<hr class="pv-rule" />

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="v66" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">लरटक्षवयैर्दीर्घैः समायुक्तैः सबिन्दुकैः।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Laraṭakṣavayairdīrghaiḥ samāyuktaiḥ sabindukaiḥ|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">इन्द्रादीन्कल्पयेद्ध्रस्वैस्तदस्त्राणि विचक्षणः॥६६॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Indrādīnkalpayeddhrasvaistadastrāṇi vicakṣaṇaḥ||66||</span></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><ruby>One should imagine<rp> (</rp><rt>kalpayet</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> Indra, etc. <span class="pv-note">— viz. Indra, Viṣṇu and Brahmā —</span> <span class="pv-w">(indra-ādīn)</span> endowed with the <ruby>letters 'la', 'ra', 'ṭa', 'kṣa', 'va' and 'ya'<rp> (</rp><rt>laraṭakṣavayaiḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>provided with<rp> (</rp><rt>samāyuktaiḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>long vowels<rp> (</rp><rt>dīrghaiḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(and)</em> with bindu <span class="pv-note">— i.e. 'ṁ' —</span> <span class="pv-w">(sa-bindukaiḥ)</span>. <em>(And when they are provided with)</em> <ruby>short vowels<rp> (</rp><rt>hrasvaiḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>the wise one<rp> (</rp><rt>vicakṣaṇaḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <em>(considers them to be)</em> <ruby>their weapons<rp> (</rp><rt>tad-astrāṇi</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>||66||</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">This is confirmed by the stanza 42 in the chapter 30 of Abhinavagupta's Tantrāloka. You must add these long vowels: 'ā, ī, ū, ai, au'. In this way, the first bījamantra-s would look like this: 'lāṁ, līṁ, lūṁ, laiṁ and lauṁ. The same procedure must be used with the other letters, i.e. with 'ra', 'ṭa', 'kṣa', 'va' and 'ya'.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">And when the same consonants are provided with these short vowels, viz. <ruby>'a, i, u, e and o'<rp> (</rp><rt>plus 'ṁ'</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, then the sounds arising from these combinations are regarded as the weapons of those three abovementioned gods: e.g. 'laṁ, liṁ, luṁ, leṁ and loṁ'.</p>

<hr class="pv-rule" />

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" id="v67" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">तद्वन्नासापयोभ्यां तु कल्प्यौ विष्णुप्रजापती।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Tadvannāsāpayobhyāṁ tu kalpyau viṣṇuprajāpatī|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">स्वरावाद्यतृतीयौ तु वाचकौ पद्मचक्रयोः॥६७॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Svarāvādyatṛtīyau tu vācakau padmacakrayoḥ||67||</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">इति मातृगणः प्रोक्तः सर्वकामफलप्रदः।</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Iti mātṛgaṇaḥ proktaḥ sarvakāmaphalapradaḥ|</span><br />
<span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">योगिनां योगसिद्ध्यर्थं किमन्यत्परिपृच्छसि॥६८॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Yogināṁ yogasiddhyarthaṁ kimanyatparipṛcchasi||68||</span></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><ruby>Likewise<rp> (</rp><rt>tad-vat</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <ruby>Viṣṇu and Prajāpati<rp> (</rp><rt>viṣṇu-prajāpatī</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>should be imagined<rp> (</rp><rt>kalpyau</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> as endowed with <em>(the letters for)</em> nose and milk <span class="pv-note">— i.e. 'ī' and 'ā' —</span> <span class="pv-w">(nāsā-payobhyām tu)</span>. <em>(And)</em> the first and third vowels <span class="pv-note">— i.e. 'a' and 'i' —</span> <span class="pv-w">(svarau ādya-tṛtīyau tu)</span> <em>(are)</em> <ruby>the two denoters<rp> (</rp><rt>vācakau</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> of the group of lotuses <span class="pv-note">— the entire universe —</span> <span class="pv-w">(padma-cakrayoḥ)</span>. <ruby>Thus<rp> (</rp><rt>iti</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>, <em>(this mantra)</em> <ruby>which bestows the fruits of all desires<rp> (</rp><rt>sarva-kāma-phala-pradaḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>is called<rp> (</rp><rt>proktaḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> 'Mātṛgaṇaḥ' <span class="pv-note">— lit. the host of the Mother —</span> <span class="pv-w">(mātṛ-gaṇaḥ)</span>.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><ruby>What<rp> (</rp><rt>kim</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> other <em>(thing)</em> <span class="pv-w">(anyat)</span> do you <em>(want to)</em> <ruby>ask about<rp> (</rp><rt>paripṛcchasi</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> so the yogī-s can get Yoga or Union <em>(with Me)</em> <span class="pv-w">(yoginām yoga-siddhi-artham)</span>?||67-68||</p>

<p class="pv-pair pv-c" data-pv="w1"><span class="pv-sa pv-src" lang="sa">इति श्रीमालिनीविजयोत्तरे तन्त्रे मन्त्रोद्धाराधिकारस्तृतीयः॥३॥</span><br />
<span class="pv-iast">Iti śrīmālinīvijayottare tantre mantroddhārādhikārastṛtīyaḥ||3||</span></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en"><ruby>Here ends<rp> (</rp><rt>iti</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> the third chapter dealing <ruby>with 'the selection or extraction of mantra-s'<rp> (</rp><rt>mantra-uddhāra-adhikāraḥ tṛtīyaḥ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> <ruby>in venerable Mālinīvijayottaratantra<rp> (</rp><rt>śrī-mālinī-vijaya-uttare tantre</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby><strong>||3||</strong></p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">Those combinations of vowels are described by Abhinavagupta in the stanzas 43, 44 and first half of the 45 in the chapter 30 of his Tantrāloka. <ruby>Such vowels<rp> (</rp><rt>ī, ā, a, i</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> must be repeated <ruby>in conjunction with adverbs of the jāti class<rp> (</rp><rt>viz. svāhā, vaṣaṭ, vauṣaṭ, hūm̐, namaḥ, phaṭ</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby>. All these six adverbs are used during the aṅganyāsa while placing the hands on six specific points: head, eyes, top of the head, shoulders, heart and arms.</p>

<p class="pv-en" lang="en">Finally, Śiva, after his long and cryptic description of the mantra-s to be selected, asked His Consort (His Supreme Power) about other questions She can have. Obviously, all these questions bringing His profound answers are always for the yogī-s to be able to become fully liberated Yogī-s, i.e. fully conscious of their essential identity with the Great Lord. This is the purport.</p>
