# Glossary of terms

*This dictionary is not in the treatise. It is compiled from those very Sanskrit annotations
that appear in parentheses next to each Russian word: annotations in the translation of the analysis
**2 232**, and at the top by frequency, as always, function words — `iti`, `eva`,
`tad`, `ca`. Here is left what the reader stumbles over.*

> **Whose work this is.** Kṣemarāja's analysis has been translated **for this site**: the
> source has no Russian version, only English. But **the twenty
> sūtras themselves** are a translation **by Gabriel Pradīpaka**, taken ready-made from him, [from the page
> of the twenty sūtras](https://www.sanskrit-trikashaivism.com/ru/pratyabhijnahrdayam-only-the-20-sutra-s-trika-scriptures-non-dual-shaivism-of-kashmir-ru/541). The Sanskrit — in Devanāgarī and transliteration — is also his.
> The interpretations in this dictionary were written here and are not presented as his words.

The list of this book is its own, not shared with its neighbors, and this has been determined by measurement.
“Tantrasāra](/en/ksh/tantrasara/glossary/)” takes its vocabulary from
“Tantrāloka](/en/ksh/ta/glossary/)” entirely — it is the same book, condensed by the same
author. Here the author is different, Kṣemarāja, and the book is different: out of the one hundred and twenty
sections of the “Tantrāloka,” **thirty** do not occur in it even once — the entire ritual, —
and its most frequent word, `citi`, is entirely absent from that vocabulary.

The note here is twenty times smaller than that of the *Tantrāloka*, and the last column rests on a fine count: for some articles, the reference comes out as one, not three. This is not a malfunction, but all that the book says about the word.

*The reverse path also exists: a Sanskrit word in the translation's interlinear gloss leads
here by itself.*

*The ♪ button pronounces the term. The sound is synthesized in advance from the Devanagari spelling using the voice **Lekha** (hi_IN) — this is not a recording of a reader and not a reference for pronunciation: be careful with aspirated, retroflex, and long vowels.*

*The voice reads according to the rules of Hindi, and Hindi swallows the final short "a":
`शिव` he pronounced "śiv". Therefore, to a word ending in a consonant, a
visarga is appended — and the voice pronounces the nominative case, `śivaḥ`, by which the word is
named aloud. The only price for this is: at the end a slight aspiration is heard,
which is not present in the spelling above the button.*
