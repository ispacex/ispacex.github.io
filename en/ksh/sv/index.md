# Śivastotrāvalī

*Śivastotrāvalī — "a string of hymns to Śiva," twenty hymns by Utpaladeva*

> — the Devanagari and IAST have been transferred from there unchanged, along with the entire Sanskrit
> apparatus of **Gabriel Pradīpaka**. **The translation is from two different lineages**, and the pages
> name them individually, with stanza numbers: [why so](#kin).

## What is this text

Śivastotrāvalī (Śiva-stotra-āvalī — "Śiva-hymn-string", a string of hymns to Śiva) —
a well-known collection of hymns composed by the great sage **Utpaladeva** (10th century).
Some of them were gathered by himself, some — by his disciples and others. In total, this scripture
contains twenty hymns, 450 stanzas. The venerable Kṣemarāja described in detail the composition
of this work in his "Vivṛtti" — a thorough commentary on the "Śivastotrāvalī".

Who is Utpaladeva? He is the famous disciple of Somānanda. Somānanda is the sage
who wrote the “Śivadṛṣṭi](/en/ksh/scriptures/),” the foundational scripture of the
Pratyabhijñā section within Trika Shaivism; in this section, the teachings of Trika receive a rational
justification, whereas in the Āgama and Spanda sections they were presented more
dogmatically. The son and chief disciple of Somānanda is Utpaladeva, the celebrated author
of the “Īśvarapratyabhijñā-kārikā,” the principal scripture of the same section.

And — if the translator's memory serves — Utpaladeva was the only sage
of this system who composed works of devotion in honor of Śiva. The Trika Śaivism is very
speculative; Utpaladeva wanted to add to it what is called devotion, and
thereby enrich it.

After four hundred pages of the “Parātriśikā-vivaraṇa](/en/ksh/pv/)” and twenty-two chapters
of the “Tantrasāra](/en/ksh/tantrasara/)” this is a change of breath: not a treatise, but verses. Short
stanzas, direct speech to Śiva, no scholasticism.

## Whose work is this translation {#kin}

Two different ones, and the difference between them is essential. It is the assembly that counts them, not a person:
6 stanzas and 444.

**Stanzas 1.1–1.6 are translated from the English exposition** of Gabriel Pradīpaka — just as
the entire section «[Paratriśika-vivaraṇa](/en/ksh/pv/)». Between the Sanskrit and the Russian
stands his work: he analyzed the stanza, labeled each word, and added explanations.

**The remaining 444 are translated directly from Sanskrit, here.** There was no other way: the source has a single word under them — *Untranslated*. No English rendering exists for them, nothing to rely on. All twenty hymns, all 450 stanzas, are translated.

What this means for the reader should be said directly:

* there is nothing to check this translation against. Two complete English translations
  of the “Śivastotrāvalī” exist — by N. K. Kotru (Motilal Banarsidass, 1985) and
  by Constance Rhodes Bailey (SUNY Press, 1987) — but both are under copyright, and
  they were not used in this work. There is no Russian translation of these hymns at
  all: another book by Utpaladeva, “Recognition of Īśvara,” has been translated into
  Russian;
* in open access there is only the Sanskrit — the edition from the Kashmir Series of
  Texts and Studies with Kṣemarāja’s commentary, [on archive.org](https://archive.org/details/ShivaStotravaliRajanakLakshman),
  CC0. There is no translation in it, but it can be used to verify the Sanskrit itself
  where the reading is disputed;
* therefore the translation is made as a literal gloss, not as smooth verse: above
  each Russian word stands the Sanskrit word it renders. Anyone who knows the language
  even a little can check it line by line, without looking anywhere else;
* where the reading is doubtful, this is stated in place, <span class="pv-note">— with a mark
  like this —</span>, and not glossed over with a pretty phrase.

The caption under each page names both genealogies with stanza numbers—
and it cannot diverge from what lies on the page.

## Why is this here

Then, for the same reason the "Tantrasāra](/en/ksh/tantrasara/)" was transferred: [site search](/search/) searches the pages of this repository, not others. The text left at the link is not in the index at all — a query for "*bhakti*" or "*saṁvinmaya*" simply will not find the "Śivastotrāvalī", even though it is there.

Moreover, neighboring pages refer to these hymns. In [«Tantrāloka» 13.290](/ksh/ta/ch13/#t13.290)
Abhinavagupta quotes in full the stanza 13.11 — the one where Utpaladeva asks the Lord
why He delays the Revelation — and until now there was nowhere to refer to in response.
Now every stanza has its own address: [/ksh/sv/ch13/#v13.11](/ksh/sv/ch13/#v13.11).

## What these hymns are about

The goal of life is Liberation. Throughout his history, man has always sought freedom, but, according to the teaching of Trika, this is not true Liberation: the true one is not about the body leaving some prison. True Liberation is equivalent to attaining His *Svātantrya* — Absolute Freedom. When the Svātantrya of the Great Lord is attained, one sees unity in everything, that is, one ceases to see duality as one did before. Everything is always identified with Svātantrya, with Him — and this ends the story called "you are in captivity." From that moment, nothing will stand in your way: if something seems to stand, it is again Svātantrya. This unceasing awareness of unity in everything is true Freedom. There is no higher attainment.

Keeping this in mind, now read the "Śivastotrāvalī" and experience the Supreme Bliss, dear
Śiva.

*Thus the introduction to the scripture is set forth by Gabriel Pradīpaka; here it is rendered
into Russian.*
