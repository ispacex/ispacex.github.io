## What is this text

Hello, again Gabriel Pradīpaka <span class="pv-note">— incorrect spelling: Pradipaka —</span>.
Parātrīśikāvivaraṇa <span class="pv-note">— incorrect spelling: Paratrishika Vivarana —</span> is
an explanation, or exposition <span class="pv-w">(vivaraṇa)</span>, made by Abhinavagupta on
Parātrīśikā (the last part of the revered "Rudrayāmala"). In Trika, eleven tantras are revered. Of them
five are the main ones: Mālinīvijaya (also called Mālinīvijayottara), Vijñānabhairava, Svacchanda,
Rudrayāmala and Netra (also known as Mṛtyuñjit). This text is also called Parātriṁśaka or
Parātriṁśikā. Therefore, the explanation by Abhinavagupta on the last part of the "Rudrayāmala" can be called
in three different ways: Parātrīśikāvivaraṇa, Parātriṁśakavivaraṇa
<span class="pv-note">— incorrect spelling: Paratrimshaka Vivarana —</span> or Parātriṁśikāvivaraṇa
<span class="pv-note">— incorrect spelling: Paratrimshika Vivarana —</span>.

Who is Abhinavagupta? The simplest way to put it is this: he is the greatest Master of the Trika philosophy. He
wrote about forty books, of which the most extensive and the most important is the *Tantrāloka*. In any case,
the present *Parātrīśikāvivaraṇa* is considered the most difficult writing to translate and understand in the entire
Trika system—and you will soon see that this label has good reason. This writing is "originally"
intended for very advanced students, as well as for jīvanmukta <span class="pv-note">—those liberated
while alive—</span>, and I am not joking in the least. Hence it is obvious that I will have to add lengthy
explanations, otherwise the reader's boat will be shipwrecked before even leaving the shore.

Parātrīśikā is also known as Trikasūtra — the aphorisms of Trika — because it contains teachings that
form the very core of this philosophical system. The themes of this monumental treatise, as
always, are the Supreme Reality and how to realize It, but here Abhinavagupta places emphasis on mantra,
as the reader will yet see. Abhinavagupta's style has always been difficult for most
translators to understand, but this time the bar has been raised to a new height. So for me personally, this will ultimately
be a journey into madness — especially because, added to the difficult translation, there is heavy work:
making the scripture comprehensible (if viewed optimistically) to the ordinary reader through endless explanations.
In short, our voyage, dear reader, may easily turn into the voyage of the mythical "Titanic,"
if we are not careful enough, ha-ha. Well then, to work!

## How to read these pages

Each part is structured the same way: first the Sanskrit, then the transliteration, then the translation, and after
it — a detailed explanation by the translator.

* <span class="pv-sa">Green</span> marks the Sanskrit of Abhinavagupta himself,
  <span class="pv-sa pv-src">reddish</span> — the original verses of Śiva and Śakti, that is,
  the “Parātriśikā”.
* Next comes the same verse in IAST transliteration — in *italics*.
* The translation is separated by a vertical line on the left. In it, <span class="pv-w">(in parentheses)</span> stands
  the Sanskrit word to which the adjacent Russian word refers. A word that has an article
  in the [glossary of terms](/en/ksh/pv/glossary/) is a link there; from the glossary, links lead back to
  the places where the term is discussed.
* *Italics in parentheses* — words added by the translator to make the phrase coherent;
  <span class="pv-note">— like this —</span> marks clarifications not present in the original.

## About the Source

Sanskrit (Devanāgarī and IAST) is carried over unchanged from
[sanskrit-trikashaivism.com](https://www.sanskrit-trikashaivism.com/ru/paratrishika-vivarana-introduction-trika-scriptures-non-dual-shaivism-of-kashmir-ru/540),
where Gabriel Pradīpaka translates the treatise from Sanskrit into English and provides it with explanations.
The Russian text on these pages is a translation of his English exposition, made for this site;
for the exact meaning and for the Sanskrit, one should always go to the source.

Among Russian translations, it is worth keeping at hand [Tantrasāra](/en/ksh/tantrasara/)
and [Basic Principles of KŚ](/en/ksh/principles/) — they provide the minimum set of concepts without which
the “Parātriśikā-vivaraṇa” is unreadable.
