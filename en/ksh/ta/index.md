# Тантралока

"The Light of the Tantras" by Abhinavagupta (c. 950–1020) is a compendium of the entire teaching of Trika and the most extensive work of the tradition: **37 chapters, 5,849 stanzas**, more than half of the entire [collection of scriptures](/en/ksh/scriptures/) by volume.

This is a local copy — so that a stanza can be found by any word and
referenced by its number. The first **16 chapters (3,988 stanzas)** are laid out here
in full: Devanagari, transliteration, and Russian translation with each stanza.
The rest are in transliteration only.

The translation and the entire Sanskrit apparatus belong to **[Gabriel
Pradīpaka](https://www.sanskrit-trikashaivism.com/)** and have been transferred from his website
without changes: not a single line of the translation here has been done by us. In Russian, he has
chapters 1–16 ready, and the work continues — chapters 17–37 are so far readable only
in English, and from each of them there is a link there.

## How to read these pages

In chapters 1–16, each stanza is accompanied by <span class="pv-sa">Devanagari</span>, beneath it *the same stanza in IAST transliteration*, and following that — a translation. In the translation, the Sanskrit word is placed <ruby>as a subscript<rp> (</rp><rt>saṁskṛta</rt><rp>)</rp></ruby> above its Russian equivalent, rather than in parentheses within the line: a parenthesis after every second word tore the sentence apart. The parentheses, however, have not disappeared — they remain visible when copying and in search.

The word that has an article in the **[glossary of terms](/en/ksh/ta/glossary/)** —
is a link there; from the glossary, links lead back, directly to the paragraphs where the term
is discussed most thoroughly. The explanations in the glossary are written for this site: the translation
here is someone else's, but the explanations are our own, and they are not presented as translation.

The translation is not placed at every stanza, but at a **group**: however many stanzas the source has combined into one paragraph, to that many his translation applies. Most often a group contains a single stanza, and then the number of the translation coincides with the number of the stanza above it; for a group, the number is shown as a range — “1.116–121”.

## Where does this come from

The transliteration is taken from the collection `Trika_Scriptures_IAST_transliteration` — it
is based on the "IAST only" versions of the site
[sanskrit-trikashaivism.com](https://www.sanskrit-trikashaivism.com/en/node/696)
and contains only Sanskrit. Devanagari and the Russian translation have been transferred from the pages
of the same site without changes; their author is **Gabriel Pradīpaka**. What is and is not in the collection in Russian — see the page [“Writings of Trika”](/en/ksh/scriptures/).

One correction has been made to the Sanskrit, and it is of the kind visible only upon collation: in the 15th chapter, the 444th stanza is labeled "441" in the source, although the 441st stands two groups above. The number is taken by position. Everything else matched the collection sign for sign—1403 groups out of 1405, and the remaining two differ in that in the collection, the introductory line of the 3rd and 16th chapters is attached to the first stanza; here it is separated.

The pages are collected `tools/trika/build-ta.py` and are edited not by hand, but by it.
