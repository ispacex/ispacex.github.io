## Nāṭyaśāstra

[Search by chapters](/dance/search) · [Glossary of terms](/en/dance/glossary)

Updates will be published in the [channel](https://t.me/natyashastra).

The translation of some chapters is based on [the NŚ website](https://natyashastra.narod.ru/) with minor corrections, the original source being [Wisdomlib](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/the-natyashastra), English translation by Manomohan Ghosh.

* [Chapter 1](/en/dance/ns-ch1), the genesis of drama;
* [Chapter 2](/en/dance/ns-ch2), characteristics of the building for performances;
* [Chapter 3](/en/dance/ns-ch3), worship of the gods of the stage (raṅgapūjā);
* [Chapter 4](/en/dance/ns-ch4), on dance;
* [Chapter 5](/en/dance/ns-ch5) describes the ritual of pūrvaraṅga – the preparation of the stage;
* [Chapter 6](/en/dance/ns-ch6) is devoted to the theory of Rasa [effects of performing arts];
* [Chapter 7](/en/dance/ns-ch7) reveals the concept of bhāva [emotion];
* [Chapter 8](/en/dance/ns-ch8) speaks of facial expressions and glances;
* [Chapter 9](/en/dance/ns-ch9) describes gestures [hastas] (single, combined, dance);
* [Chapter 10](/en/dance/ns-ch10) describes movements of the limbs and positions of the feet;
* [Chapter 11](/en/dance/ns-ch11) explains the basic Cārī (movements), standing positions (Sthānas) and positions with weapons;
* [Chapter 12](/en/dance/ns-ch12) describes combined movements (Maṇḍala);
* [Chapter 13](/en/dance/ns-ch13) speaks of various gaits;
* [Chapter 14](/en/dance/ns-ch14) explains how local theatrical customs should be taken into account;
* [Chapter 15](/en/dance/ns-ch15) – verbal representation (vācika) and prosody;
* [Chapter 16](/en/dance/ns-ch16) – metrical patterns (chandas);
* [Chapter 17](/en/dance/ns-ch17) – the language of the play (lakṣaṇa);
* [Chapter 18](/en/dance/ns-ch18) – rules for the use of languages (bhāṣā);
* [Chapter 19](/en/dance/ns-ch19) – forms of address and intonation;
* [Chapter 20](/en/dance/ns-ch20) – ten kinds of drama (daśarūpa);
* [Chapter 21](/en/dance/ns-ch21) – members of parts (sandhi);
* [Chapter 22](/en/dance/ns-ch22) – styles (vṛtti);
* [Chapter 23](/en/dance/ns-ch23) – costumes and makeup (nepathya);
* [Chapter 24](/en/dance/ns-ch24) – harmonious representation (sāmānyābhinaya);
* [Chapter 25](/en/dance/ns-ch25) – dealing with courtesans (vaiśika);
* [Chapter 26](/en/dance/ns-ch26) – pictorial representation (citrābhinaya);
* [Chapter 27](/en/dance/ns-ch27) – success in a dramatic production (siddhi);
* [Chapter 28](/en/dance/ns-ch28) – on instrumental music (ātodya);
* [Chapter 29](/en/dance/ns-ch29) – on stringed instruments (tata);
* [Chapter 30](/en/dance/ns-ch30) – on hollow instruments (suṣira);
* [Chapter 31](/en/dance/ns-ch31) – on the measure of time (tāla);
* [Chapter 32](/en/dance/ns-ch32) – on dhruvā songs;
* [Chapter 33](/en/dance/ns-ch33) – on covered instruments (avanaddha);
* [Chapter 34](/en/dance/ns-ch34) – types of characters (prakṛti);
* [Chapter 35](/en/dance/ns-ch35) – on the distribution of roles (bhūmikā);
* [Chapter 36](/en/dance/ns-ch36) – on the descent of drama to the earth.

Most lists contain 36 chapters; however, there are sources where chapters 37 and 38 are present.

* [Chapter 37](/en/dance/ns-ch37) tells the story of a king who sent his sons to Earth to impart Nāṭya to people.
