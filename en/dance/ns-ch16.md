Uneven and semi-even meters

115/ Such, O best of brahmins, are the even meters, which I have set forth earlier. Now hear about the uneven and half-even meters.

116/ Meters in which the feet belong to different metrical types and are not similar to each other are called uneven (viṣama).

117-118/ Meters in which two alternating feet are similar, while two adjacent feet are not similar to each other, are called half-even (ardhasama).

119/ An even meter is defined by the definition of one of its feet, whereas an uneven meter requires the definition of all its feet.

120/ In half-even meters, odd feet do not contain the gaṇa "ja", while even feet may vary.

Pathya

121-122/ Patya -- Anuṣṭubh class meter: the feet use the gaṇas sa, sa, ga, ga and sa, ra, la, ga.

123-124/ Uneven Pāṭhya -- a fully uneven variety: each foot uses a different combination of gaṇas.

125-126/ Inverted Pāṭhya -- the second and fourth feet are inverted: the characteristics are swapped compared to the first and third feet.

127-128/ Chapala -- eight syllables in each foot: the fourth, fifth, and sixth syllables are short.

129-131/ Vipulā -- eight syllables in each foot: the seventh syllable is short in certain feet.

134-135/ Vaktra -- eight syllables in each foot: three heavy syllables at the end.

137-138/ Ketumatī -- four-foot meter: the feet consist of the gaṇas sa, ja, sa, ga and bha, ra, na, ga, ga.

139-140/ Udgata -- a four-foot metre: the feet contain the gaṇas sa, ja, sa, la; na, sa, ja, ga; bha, na, ja, la, ga; sa, ja, sa, ja, ga.

141-142/ Lalita -- a four-foot metre: the feet contain the gaṇas sa, ja, sa, la; na, sa, ja, ga; na, na, sa, sa; sa, ja, sa, ja, ga.

143-144/ Aparavaktra -- a four-foot metre: odd feet -- na, na, ra, la, ga; even feet -- na, ja, ja, ra.

145-146/ Puṣpitagrā -- a four-foot metre: odd feet -- na, na, ra, ya; even feet -- na, ja, ja, ra, ga.

147-148/ Vanavāsika -- gāthā in sixteen mātrās, divided into four sections using gaṇas and partial triads.

The sizes of the Āryā type

152/ Thus have I briefly set forth the definitions of the various syllabic meters. Next, I shall give the definitions of the meters of the Āryā type.

153/ Ārya is of five kinds: Pathya, Vipulā, Chapalā, Mukha-chapalā and Jaghana-chapalā.

155-159/ General structure of the Āryā: gaṇas of four mātrās; the second and fourth feet are even; the first and third -- are odd; the eighth gaṇa -- is "half" (two mātrās); for the sixth gaṇa variants are possible: "ja" or four short syllables; the placement of the caesura follows certain rules.

160-161/ Pathya-Āryā: caesura after three gaṇas; twelve mātrās in odd feet.

162-163/ Vipula-Āryā: without caesura; odd gaṇas with long syllables; even gaṇas -- long in the middle.

164-165/ Chapala-Āryā: the even gaṇas (second, fourth) consist of the type "ja".

166-167/ Mukha-capala-Āryā: the definition of capala applies only to the first half-verse.

168/ Jaghana-capala-Āryā: the definition of Capala applies only to the second half-verse.

169/ Sarvatash-capala-Ārya: the signs of Capala are visible in both half-verses.

170-171/ The total number of mātṛs: the first half-verse -- thirty; the second half-verse -- twenty-seven.
