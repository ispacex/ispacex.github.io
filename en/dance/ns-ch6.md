**ДА БУДЕТ БЛАГО**

**NĀṬYA ŚĀSTRA**

**/THE SCIENCE OF THEATRE/**

**applied by the enlightened Bharata**

So, the sixth chapter of **Nāṭyaśāstra** by Bharata:

1/ Having heard the description of the prologue, the greatest in spirit spoke again. All the sages asked Bharata: "Give us an answer regarding five things:

2/ Which *rasa*s are studied in **Nāṭya?** What, truly, constitutes their essence?

3/ What are the mentioned *bhāva?* How exactly are they realized? And also, what in reality are *saṅgraha, kārikā* and *nirukta?»*

4/ Having heard those words of the sages, the holy Bharata spoke in reply about the distinctions between *bhāva* and *rasa*.

5/ “In the beginning I will tell you in full, O rich in ascetic deeds, in sequence about *saṅgraha, kārikā* and *nirukta.*

6/ There is no possibility of ever reaching the end along the path of this **Nāṭya**. It is endless, because the quantity of knowledge and skills contained within it is too great.

7/ Indeed, it is impossible to reach the limit even in one of these knowledges, for it itself is already an ocean—what then of the path that unites all knowledges, the path leading to truth?

8/ Nevertheless, I will speak of that which pertains to this **Nāṭya**. I shall begin with a description of *bhāva* and *rasa* in the form of *saṅgraha*, which contain the secret meaning in a condensed formula and comprise only conclusions.

9/ The wise should know - *saṅgraha* briefly presents that which requires detailed commentaries and explanations.

10/ *Nāṭyasaṅgraha* includes theses on *rasa, bhāva* and *abhinaya*, on the laws of classical and folk **Nāṭya**, on the different styles of **Nāṭya** and its variations in various countries, on the success of **Nāṭya**, on musical tones and the orchestra, as well as on singing and the form of the stage.

11/ *Karika* is that which briefly describes some subordinate question.

12/ *Nirukta*, however, examines various proper names, relying on a dictionary of Vedic words and scholarly books, uncovering the meanings of word roots and their inflected parts.

13/ *Nirukta* represents a synthesis of many fields of knowledge, including the numerology of roots.

14/ O best of the twice-born, what I state first in brief will then be told with *nirukta* and *kārikā*.

15/ **Love, Laughter, Sorrow, Anger, Heroism, Fear, Disgust** and **Wonder** — these are the eight kinds of traditional *rasa* in **Nāṭya**.

16/ It is precisely these eight that were proclaimed by the Tamer of Rebels, the Great Spirit (Brahmā). Now, I shall speak of *bhāva* – *sthāyibhāva, sañcāribhāva* and *sattvabhāva.*

17/ **Delight, jest** and **anguish, fury, might,** as well as **fear, disgust** and **amazement** — these are the well-known *sthāyibhāva.*

18/ **Despair, emptiness, suspicion,** also **envy, intoxication** and **fatigue, laziness** and **dejection,** **preoccupation** and **fainting,** **recollection** and **contentment,**

** **19/** shame, irascibility, joy **and **confusion, **as well as **stupidity, pride,** **disappointment, languor, drowsiness and obsession,

20/ **sleep, awakening** and **irritation**, as well as **deception** and **punishment**, **prudence, illness, madness** and **dying,**

21/ and also **fear** and **supposition** should be known as *sañcāri* or *vyabhicāribhāva*. These states are named 33 in number.

22/ **Numbness, perspiration, horripilation**, **change of voice, trembling, pallor, tears** and **unconsciousness** — these are, according to tradition, the eight *sattvabhāva.*

23/ **Aṅgika** (body), **Vācika** (voice), **Āhārya** (costume), and **Sāttvika** (truthfulness of existence) – these are the four well-known *abhinaya* connected with **Nāṭya.**

24/ **Lokadharmi** (folk theatre) and **Nāṭyadharmi** (classical theatre) – these are the two traditions.

25/ **Bhārati**, **sāttvati**, **kaiśiki** and **ārabhaṭi*** – *these are the 4 *vṛtti* (styles) constituting the essence of **Nāṭya**.

**Avanti, dakṣiṇātyā,*** ***udhrāmagadhī*** *and **pañcālamadhyamā ***—these are the well-known *pravṛtti* (varieties).

**Daivikī** (the arrangement of the gods) and **mānuṣī** (human will) are the two factors of *siddhi* (success).

26/7 sounds, sung and played – **ṣaḍja** (born of six), **niṣāda** (seated), **ṛṣabha** (bull), **gāndhāra** (prince), **madhya** (middle), **pañcama** (fifth), **dhaivata** (steadfast).

**Tata** (strings), also **avanaddha** (percussion), **ghana** (idiophones) and **suṣira** (wind instruments) –

27/ 4 known types of instruments that make up the orchestra. Strings, as is known, reveal themselves through the sound of the string, while percussion instruments belong to the air space,

28/ percussion instruments keep the rhythm, while wind instruments, as is known, are made of bamboo. **Praveśa** (during a character's entrance), **akṣepa** (demonstration of *rasa*), **niṣkrama** (during an exit), **prasādika** (for attaining divine grace), and **antara** (for expressing an inner state) –

29/ such are the five known types of singing used for composing **dhruva.**

Three kinds of stage are known - square, rectangular, and triangular.

30/ Thus, this *nāṭyasaṅgraha*, consisting of brief statements, has been demonstrated.

Now I will set forth the main principles in the form of *sūtra*.

First of all, we will speak about *rasa* from the very beginning.

For there is no meaning in **Nāṭya** without *rasa*. *Rasa*, in turn, arises through the realization of *vibhāva, anubhāva and vyabhicāri.*

And what example is there of this?

And here is what is said: just as from the combination of various spices, herbs, and other substances the taste of a dish is obtained, so too from the entering into various *bhāva* is born *rasa*. Or just as from the combination of molasses with other substances, spices, and herbs the six tastes arise, so too do *sthāyibhāva*, passing through different *bhāva*, attain the quality of *rasa*.

Now the question arises: why is this called "*rasa* (i.e., taste)"?

It is said, because of tasting. In what way is *rasa* tasted? Here it is said: for just as food enriched with many seasonings possesses flavor, and a discerning person, tasting it, delights, likewise,

tasting *sthāyibhāva, seasoned with various *bhāva *and *abhinaya,* and accompanied by inspired speech and bodily movements, the spectator capable of empathy attains the rapture of delight. Hence the name – *nāṭyarasa* (the delight of feeling born of **Nāṭya**).

There are two ślokas on this, handed down from generation to generation:

32/ Just as people who know their food enjoy the taste of food that has various ingredients and seasonings,

33/ so do the wise delight their soul with those *sthāyibhāva* that arise through *abhinaya* of various *vyabhicāri*. Therefore, from ancient times this is called *nāṭyarasa* (the tasting of **Nāṭya**).

Now the question arises — do *bhāva* arise from *rasa*, or *rasa* from *bhāva*? Some hold that they are realised through their mutual conjunction. But why? For it is evident that *bhāva* do not arise from *rasa*, but rather from *bhāva* are born *rasa*.

There are ślokas about this:

34/ The knowers of **Nāṭya** assert: *bhāva,* manifested through *abhinaya,* create *rasa*.

35/ Just as the uniqueness of a seasoning is created by combining various components according to many instructions, so too do the *bhavas* manifested through *abhinaya* give rise to *rasa*.

36/ Without *bhāva* there is no *rasa*, and free of *rasa* there is no *bhāva*. The interaction of these two is the success of the performance.

37/ And just as a combination of spices and herbs imparts flavor to food, so too do *bhāva* and *rasa* mutually create each other.

38/ Just as from a seed a tree necessarily arises, and from the tree a flower and fruit, so too *rasa* is the foundation, and *bhāva* are dependent on them.

Now we shall discuss such phenomena connected with *rasa* as their origin, color, and deity. Four *rasa* are the cause of the origin of the others, and these are **Love, Anger, Heroism**, and **Disgust.**

Now,

39/ Precisely from **Love** comes **Laughter**, from **Anger** – *rasa* **Suffering**, the source of **Wonder** is **Heroism**, and **Fear** – from **Disgust**.

40/ Imitation of **Love** evokes **Laughter**, and the result of **Anger** is known as the *rasa* of **Sorrow**.

As a result of a heroic deed, wonder arises, and the one who looks at the repulsive experiences fear.

Now the colors:

42/ The dark one is the color of **Love**, white appears as **Laughter**, gray is **Suffering**, and red manifests **Wrath**,

43/ **Heroism** is known as brilliant (light), and **Fear** as black, **Disgust** is blue in color, and **Wonder** is traditionally yellow.

Now the deities:

44/ **Love** is revered by Vishnu, the deity of **Laughter** is Pramatha, **Anger** is patronized by Rudra, the deity of **Suffering** is Yama,

45/ Mahākāla (Śiva in the form of the world's destroyer) relates to **Disgust**, the god of **Fear** is Kāla (time), the great Indra is the god of **Heroism**, the deity of **Wonder** is Brahmā.

Thus, information has been given about the origin, colors, and deities of these *rasa*.

Now we will speak about the distinguishing features of *anubhāva, vibhāva* and *vyabhicāri.* We will also explain the *sthāyibhāva* of the various *rasa*.

So, the sentiment called **Love** originates from the *sthāyibhāva ***delight**. The costume suitable for **Love** is luxurious, the best in the world, pure, sacred, radiant, and beautiful. It is said that one whose costume is luxurious is himself the embodiment of **Love**.

Just as the names of people are determined by the way of life of the clan-tribe in which they are born, so too are the names of *rasa, bhāva* and everything connected with **Nāṭya** determined by their purpose and correspond to their behavior.

That is why for *rasa ***Love** the style of behavior dictates an expensive and very beautiful costume. **Love** is the cause of the bond between man and woman; its character is youth and nobility.

**Love** has two states – satisfied and deceived.

Satisfied **Love** is born from the following *vibhāvas:* spring, flowers, ointments and ornaments, walks in the grove together with the desired person, and the seeing and hearing of everything that awakens desire and gives pleasure, as well as playful games and the like. It should be represented through the following *anubhāvas:* skillful glances with the raising of the brows, coquettish sidelong looks at one's partner, pleasantness in gestures and speech, and the like. But *vyabhicāris* such as fear, languor, terror, and disgust are excluded.

Deceived **Love**, however, should be represented by such *anubhāva*s as despair, exhaustion, suspicion, indignation, weariness, absorption in passionate longing, sleep and waking from sleep, the illness of madness and intoxication, languor, cooling, death, and the like.

Here it is asked — if **the enjoyment of *sthāyibhāva* of Love**, then why

But are its *bhāva* present in what is connected with **Suffering**?

Further it is said: it has already been stated earlier that **Love** is of two kinds, satisfied and deceived (although the creator of the "Science of Erotics" (*vaiśika śāstra*) has named 10 of its aspects). We, however, will speak of that which unites the *abhinaya* of these two *rasa*.

So, *bhāva ***Suffering** is a state of hopelessness arising from a curse, illness, death of a loved one, loss of wealth, ruin, or captivity. Whereas deceived love is a state of dependence arising from focus on passionate desire.

Thus do **Suffering** and deceived **Love** differ from one another. In like manner, this feeling of **Love** is connected with all *bhāva*.

И ещё,

46/ Here is a devotee against the backdrop of blooming spring, full of happiness, or a certain husband united with a young beauty — thus is **Love** represented.

47/ *Rasa ***Love** is aroused by garlands of spring flowers, the enjoyment of poetry and music together with the beloved, as well as by amusements and strolls in groves.

48/ This should be presented as follows — sweet glances and speeches full of joy from delightful smiles, words that bring satisfaction, pleasantness and ease of movements.

Now *rasa ***Comic**. Its nature is constituted by the *sthāyibhāva ***mirth**. Its *vibhāva *are as follows: an unsuitable costume and ornaments, as if borrowed from another's shoulder, frivolous boldness, pretense, falsehood and idle talk, failure to understand what is happening or distortion of its meaning when conveyed in words, errors in pronunciation, and the like. It should be represented by the following *anubhāva:*

mouth covered by the palm, nose and cheeks trembling, eyes squeezed shut, body hunched, face sweaty and red, hands clutching the sides. And its *vyabhicāri* – these are feigned languor, weakness, drowsiness, sleep, awakening, discontent.

*Rasa ***Laughter **also comes in two types: absorbed in oneself and occupied with another. When the actor laughs himself, then it is absorbed in oneself; when he makes another laugh, i.e., amuses, then it is occupied with another.

Now two ślokas, handed down from ancient times in the *āryā* meter:

49/ With improperly worn ornaments, walking incorrectly, saying the wrong thing, dressed inappropriately, awkward and laughing at oneself — thus is traditionally presented the ***rasa* of Laughter**.

50/ When someone makes another person laugh with absurd mannerisms, grimaces, and a strange costume—this too is known as *rasa **Laughter.***

51/ This feeling is most often shown by female and low characters. It has six degrees, which I will now describe.

52/ Smile (smita), gentle **Laughter** (hasita), loud **Laughter** (vihasita),

laughter (upahasita), loud laughter (apahasita), **Laughter** to exhaustion (atihasita). In sequence, these two degrees each correspond respectively to the elevated, middle, and lower nature.

So,

53/ A smile and cheerful **Laughter** are of the highest nature, of the middle nature are loud laughter and guffawing, while of the lowest nature are cackling and **Laughter** to the point of exhaustion, as is known.

Now the ślokas,

54/ A smile is **Laughter** of the highest nature, when the face is calm, the teeth are unnoticeable, the face radiates strength, a slight hint in the gaze, and the cheeks are slightly stretched.

55/ Cheerful **Laughter** is represented with wide-open eyes and mouth, when the teeth are visible, and the cheeks are also stretched.

56/ Loud **Laughter** should indeed be loud, but pleasant; the cheeks rise so that the eyes become narrow, and the face gradually reddens.

57/ If the nostrils are flared, the eyes look askance, and the head and shoulders are bent, then this is laughter.

58/ When **Laughter** occurs in an inappropriate place, and even before tears in the eyes and shaking shoulders and head, then it is already guffawing.

59/ A **Laughter** to the point of exhaustion is tears rolling from the eyes, indistinct sounds bursting forth, and hands clutching the sides.

60/ In those places where the performance is to be humorous, one should apply these degrees of **Laughter**, of both the highest and the middle and low nature.

61/ Thus, it is known that there are two types of **Laughter** – arising within oneself and evoked in another, three of its natures, and six stages.

Now about the *rasa ***Sorrow**: its *sthāyibhāva is ***Grief**; its *vibhāva is *a curse, torture, separation from a beloved or their death, loss of wealth, death, captivity, forced flight, or some other harm or misfortune; its *anubhāva* - falling tears, plaintive entreaty, pallor of the face, weakened body, sighs, absent-mindedness, and other such; its *vyabhicāri - *despair, exhaustion, absorption in passionate desire, agitation, dizziness, fainting, fatigue, fear, sorrow, dejection, illness, numbness, madness, nocturnal recollections, languor, weakness, dying, insensibility, trembling, pallor of the face, change of voice, and the like.

Here are two ślokas in the *āryā* meter:

62/ *Rasa ***Suffering** arises from such characteristic states as the sight of a deceased beloved or hearing unpleasant words.

63/ *Abhinaya* *rasa* ***The suffering** is as follows — loud lamentations and weeping, a semi-fainting state, as well as ritual beating of the body.

Further, *rasa ***Wrath**. Its essence is constituted by the *sthāyibhāva * **fury**, which is inherent in the nature of arrogant people, as well as of rākṣasas and dānavas, and is the cause of battles. **Wrath** is excited by such *vibhāva* as wildness, insults, humiliation of a hero, murderous speech, fury, enmity, envy, and so on. Its actions are the following: striking, breaking, causing pain, cutting, destroying, seizing weapons, overthrowing, drawing in, and fighting.

He must be represented with such *anubhāva* as red eyes, arched eyebrows, gnashing of teeth, compressed lips, trembling cheeks, and arms extended forward for a strike.

His bhavas are as follows: recklessness, ardour, agitation, indignation, impetuosity, pre-eminence, pride, trembling, hair standing on end, incoherent speech, and so on.

Now the question arises — it is said that this *rasa ***Anger ***is primarily connected with rākṣasas and dānavas. Does it have any relation to others?

It is said: yes, it does. However, this section explains that their nature indeed corresponds to **Wrath**. In what way? They have enormous arms and large heads with red hair, hostile and irritated, with red eyes that spin as if bulging from their sockets, terrible and frightful in appearance. And if someone displays such a nature, moves in such a way, and behaves in such a manner, then he will belong to the *rasa* of **Wrath**. To the *rasa* of **Love**, chiefly, refined people resort, while **Wrath**, consisting of struggle and clashes, is favored by people similar to those described above, and it is they who imitate it.

Now the ślokas, handed down from ancient times:

64/ *Rasa ***Anger **is preferable in cases of battle, struggle, killing, inflicting harm, destruction and devastation, as well as fights and disturbances.

65/ It must be performed with the following distinctive features: many battles and shootings, and various kinds of mutilation and captures.

66/ Thus, *rasa ***Anger **is shown through furious bodily movements, an abundance of weapons and battles, and possesses the nature of a cruel sacrifice.

Further, *rasa ***Heroism**. Its nature is the highest, its essence is aspiration. Its *vibhāva*: self-control, resoluteness, courtesy, strength, might, creative gift and grandeur, as well as magnificence and so on.

His *anubhāva* is firmness, wisdom, courage, self-sacrifice, learning, and the like. His *bhāva* is firmness of intention, pride, impetuosity, strength of indignation, remembrance, horripilation, and others.

Here in the *āryā* meter there are ślokas for contemplating this feeling -

67/ *Rasa ***Heroism** is born from strength of resolve, self-mastery, and imperviousness to being blinded by the wondrous.

68/ Правильно представлять *раса ***Геройство** следует так – настойчивость, мудрость, мужество, сила, гордость, рвение, могущество и величие, а также споры и брань.

Now about the *rasa ***Fear**. *Sthāyibhāva* **fear** constitutes its nature. The sight of a wildly roaring creature, or of Śiva, or of an owl, fright and anxiety when visiting an uninhabited house or being in a forest, the sight of the killing or captivity of a relative, even a heard account of this – by these and similar *vibhāva* is **Fear** excited. It should be performed with the following *anubhāva* – trembling in hands and feet, a wandering glance, the hairs on the body standing on end, pallor of the face, change of voice, and so forth.

His *bhāva* are the following – paralysis, drops of sweat, incoherent speech, shivering, trembling, change of voice, pallor, fear, fainting, dejection, agitation, now disordered movement, now stillness, fright, possession, death, and others.

Here are the ślokas in the *āryā* meter:

69/ **Fear** arises at the sight of a wildly roaring creature, while wandering in a dense forest or in an empty house, and also because of a transgression against a teacher or a ruler.

70/ **Fear** is accompanied by the appearance in the hero of changes in the eyes, face, and entire body, noticeable trembling in the numbed thighs, pallor, dryness in the mouth, and increased heartbeat.

71/ A similar nature manifests in *sattvabhāva*. Although in this case the *bhāva*s are the same, they must be expressed more weakly.

72/ **Fear** is always depicted with a parched throat, palate, and lips, with trembling in the heart, a numbed body, and shivering in the hands and feet.

Further, the *rasa* of **Disgust**, whose essence, its *sthāyibhāva*, is **aversion**. It is excited by the following *vibhāva* — mention, sight, or hearing of something ugly, displeasing, unclean, and unpleasant. It should be performed with such *anubhāva* as vomiting when depicting a corpse, a decaying body, or a wounded face. And its *bhāva* are these: the desire to forget, irritation, confusion, fainting, illness, death.

Here are the original lines in the *āryā* meter:

73/ *Rasa ***Disgust** arises from irritation and unwillingness to perceive something that has a bad smell or taste, is unpleasant to the touch or to the ear.

74/ The correct depiction of **Disgust** is as follows – eyes narrowed, mouth twisted, nose pinched, and a grimace on the face, gait unsteady.

Now the *rasa ***Wonder**, whose nature is constituted by the *sthāyibhāva* **astonishment**. It is excited by the following *vibhāva* — the sight of a wondrous person, desired or charming, upon reaching a grove where a tribe of gods and the like rests, upon entering a palace, at the sight of a celestial chariot, magic, or an assembly of many kings, etc. It should be shown through such *anubhāva* as widened eyes, a gaze like that of a ram, shivering, tears, perspiration, agitation, sincerity in words and generosity in gifts, the cry "Oh!", a widely opened mouth, trembling of the fingers, turning from side to side, and others. Its *bhāva* are numbness, tears, drops of sweat, indistinct speech, raising of the hairs on the body, excitement, confusion, loss of the ability to reason, complete absorption in the object, and the like.

Here there are pre-existing ślokas in the *ārya* meter:

75/ Everything created as outstanding—whether it be remarkable speech, extraordinary skill, or the astonishing nature of some action—all these are forms of *vibhāva*, known for the *rasa* of **Wonder**.

76/ It should be shown with such a cry, as if touching a crocodile or an owl; the words should be involuntary, and the speech confused and agitated, with the appearance of drops of sweat, etc.

77/ It should be known that the nature of **Love** manifests through three components: voice, costume, and performance. **Laughter** and **Anger**, according to tradition, also have three modes of expression—bodily movements, costume, and text.

78/ **Suffering**, according to tradition, is of three kinds – that arising from violation of the moral law, that proceeding from loss of glory, and that born of grief.

79/ *Rasa ***Heroism**, as Brahmā reports, also has three kinds – the feat of generosity, moral heroism, and heroism shown in battle.

80/ And precisely three kinds of *rasa ***Fear** are known – from perfect deception, due to carelessness, and also from cowardice.

81/ Two kinds of **Disgust** exist — that arising from shock and that from violation of purity. Violation of purity is created by refuse and insects, while shock is born of the sight of blood and similar things.

82/ The marvelous and that born of joy — two kinds are named for the *rasa* of **Wonder**. The marvelous is that born upon seeing something wondrous, while that born of joy tradition calls delight.

Now the *rasa* called **Peace**, whose nature consists of such a *sthāyibhāva* as self-control. It is the foundation of final liberation. It is excited by such *vibhāva* as true knowledge, asceticism, purity of heart, and so on. It should be performed with the help of the following *anubhāva* — self-restraint and fasting, immersion in one's own self, contemplation of the deity, moderation, devotion, love for all beings, cognition of the creative power of God, and so on. Its *vyabhicāri* — indifference to all memories and searches for satisfaction, tirelessness, honesty, numbness, raising of the hairs, and so on.

Here are ślokas in the *āryā* meter:

_/ *Rasa ***Peace **arises from immersion in oneself for the sake of final liberation, it is connected with true knowledge of goals and causes and shows the path to bliss.

_/ *Rasa*, known as **Peace**, is achieved under the following conditions: restraint of the sense organs and organs of action, the spirit rests in itself, the desire for the good and happiness of all living beings.

_/ This feeling of deep **Peace** is equally suited to all who have neither suffering, nor happiness, nor hatred, nor envy.

_/ The state that is to be attained from the feeling of **Peace** is designated as union with one's own self. All states, beginning with **pleasure**, are changing states, while **Peace** is the presumed prime cause of the world of objects. And all these changing states, being born from the cause, dissolve back into it. Every "self" possessed of impulse attains changing states, emerging from **Peace**.

_/ And again, upon the destruction of impulses, **Peace** absorbs everything.

Thus, the experts of nāṭya have shown nine *rasa*s and given their description.

83/ Thus, having understood the described knowledge of the eight *rasa*, I shall define the concept of *bhāva*.

Thus ends the sixth chapter in the **Nāṭyaśāstra** of Bharata, called **"the distinction of rasa"**.
