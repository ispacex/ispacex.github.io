* [Overview S.M. 3](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8qrXJmN0ejA)
    - ■ PR108
    - ◑ PR3 - naphthol (C17H13N3O3)
        - low weather resistance
    - □ PR12 - naphthol (C25H20N4O4)
        - low weather resistance
        - violet
    - ◑ PR188 - naphthol (C33H24Cl2N4O6)
        - weather resistance somewhat better
        - warmer than cadmium red
    - □ PR206 - quinacridone (C20H12N2O2)
        - high light- and weatherfastness
        - when lightened, resembles burnt umber or English red (PR101)
    - □ PR209 - quinacridone (C20H10Cl2O2)
        - lightfastness lower than PR206
        - mixes poorly in oil
    - ◑ PR214 - azo pigment (C40H22Cl6O4)
        - good lightfastness and intensity
    - ◑ PR242 - azo pigment (C42H22Cl4F6N6)
        - lightens toward the warm side
        - low intensity
    - ■ PR251 - azo pigment (C25H13Cl2N5O3)
        - shifts toward orange when lightened
        - high light- and weatherfastness
    - □ PR259 - aluminum-sodium sulfo-silicate (pink ultramarine)
        - weak intensity, similar to PR12
        - high light- and weatherfastness
    - ■ PR105 - lead minium (Pb3O4)
        - orange
        - toxic
        - opaque
        - mixed with viridian resembles Indian yellow
        - "denser" than PO20 (cadmium orange)
        - with ultramarine turns brown
        - used in the 14th-19th centuries
    - ■ PR106 - mercury sulfide HgS, cinnabar
        - inferior in brightness to cadmium red
        - with green phthalocyanine turns brown
        - conducts electric current
        - used in the 14th-19th centuries
